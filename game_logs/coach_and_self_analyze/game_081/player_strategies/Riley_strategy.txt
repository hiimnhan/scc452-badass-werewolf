• Delayed Confrontation: Never be the first to challenge a Seer or power role claim. If a target is identified, wait for at least two other players to express doubt before pivoting to an aggressive counter-accusation.
• Passive Alignment: Adopt the 'Mirror-Validation Loop'. Avoid inventing new logic; instead, rephrase and amplify the suspicions already voiced by the most vocal Villagers to blend into the consensus.
• Low-Profile Participation: Limit day-phase output to one high-impact, validating statement. Over-explaining or 'fighting' a majority (as seen in Round 1) marks the player as the primary target.
• Narrative-Driven Kills: Align night eliminations with the previous day's debate. Kill the person the village was closest to exiling to make the wolf attack look like a village 'success' or a misguided attempt to protect a suspected ally.
• Diversionary Planting: In mid-game, if a wolf is the primary suspect, introduce a secondary 'quiet' suspect to split the vote and prevent an exile threshold from being met.
