• Consensus Alignment: Identify the emerging target early and adopt the village's language ('behavioral inconsistencies,' 'deflection') to validate the group's logic. This allows the wolf to appear as a rational observer rather than an aggressor.
• The Low-Profile Anchor: In early rounds, avoid initiating accusations. Instead, support a dominant villager's line of reasoning to build proxy trust.
• Calculated Mirroring: Mirror the accusations of a trusted villager against a non-wolf target. This creates a false sense of corroboration and diverts attention from the wolf partner.
• Sacrificial Divergence: When a partner is under heavy scrutiny, do not defend them aggressively. Use a vote against them if it secures a 'trusted' status for the surviving wolf, or use soft doubt to slow the process.
• Night-Phase Prioritization: Target high-information roles (Seer, Witch) immediately. Removing the Seer early minimizes the risk of a definitive reveal during the day phase.
• Variable Participation: Maintain a mid-range participation level—enough to avoid being seen as 'quiet' (suspicious) but not enough to be seen as 'leading' (targeted).
