• Adaptive Initiation: In R1, avoid high-risk aggressive accusations if the table is cohesive; instead, sow doubt subtly to identify the 'alpha' villager before attacking.
• Precision Framing: Use 'pattern-based framing' (robotic/scripted labels) sparingly. Only deploy once a target shows a linguistic quirk, as overusing it early can make the Wolf look like the disruptor.
• Sacrificial Play: If the herd targets a wolf (as happened to Riley), accept the exile if it clears the remaining wolf's path to a 'trusted' status. The surviving wolf's credibility is more valuable than one wolf's survival.
• Targeted Mirroring: Instead of just mirroring logic, mirror the *suspicion* of the most influential villager to inherit their trust (Trust Anchoring).
• High-Value Elimination: Stick to the priority (Seer > Witch > Guard) but prioritize the Seer the moment they reveal a check or show too much confidence.
• Cognitive Dissonance Sowing: Create friction between suspected power roles (e.g., framing the Guard as a fake Seer) to force them to reveal themselves or fight each other.
