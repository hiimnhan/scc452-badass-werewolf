• Low-Profile Opening: Strictly avoid escalating arguments in Round 1. Aggressive counter-accusations (as seen with Nhan) trigger a 'wolf-like' pattern that unites the village. Remain observational and supportive of the initial 'reasonable' consensus.
• Strategic Agreement: Instead of fighting the majority, pivot slightly to align with the most influential player. If targeted, use a defensive but non-aggressive tone to avoid appearing disruptive.
• Shadow Partnership: Let a partner act as the primary 'disruptor' or 'noise-maker' while maintaining a facade of neutrality. Meg's silence and subsequent pivot to 'reasonable' logic were far more effective than Riley's direct confrontation.
• Targeted Logic Attacks: When pushing for an exile, don't just accuse; frame the target's behavior as a 'tactic' (e.g., 'obfuscation' or 'deflection'). Mirror the language of the villagers to make the accusation feel like a logical discovery rather than an attack.
• Endgame Convergence: In the final stages, mirror the last remaining villager's logic exactly. Convert from neutral to decisive only when the numbers guarantee victory, ensuring the villager feels you are their only remaining ally.
