Early Game (Rounds 1-3):
*   Seer: Immediately reveal ‘not a wolf’ for the first investigated player, documenting all defensive reactions. 
*   Guard: Prioritize Seer protection above all else – immediate and unwavering. Document protection decisions with specific justifications (e.g., ‘Seer’s observation of X’s behavior’). 
*   Witch: Observe accusation patterns – note who accuses whom and initial voting clusters. Avoid early probing; focus on establishing baseline behavior. 
*   Villagers: Reject ‘vibe’ accusations. Analyze voting matrices for quantifiable overlaps in behavior (simultaneous defenses, consistent agreement/disagreement). 

Mid Game (Rounds 4-6):
*   Seer: Link observations to specific player actions – ‘X consistently agrees with Y after Y initiates’. Clearly state the connection. 
*   Guard: Document *specific* protection decisions, justifying beyond ‘Seer’ – ‘Protected due to shared defensive agreement with Z’. 
*   Witch: Pose targeted questions designed to expose contradictions, focusing on accusations and justifications. Question the *basis* of accusations. 
*   Villagers: Build accusations based on interconnected behaviors, emphasizing quantifiable overlaps. Demand evidence for accusations. 

Late Game (Rounds 7+):
*   Seer: Provide direct wolf identification with unwavering certainty – ‘X is a werewolf due to Y and Z’s actions’. 
*   Guard: Maintain absolute Seer protection – no exceptions. 
*   Witch: Use poison *only* on confirmed Wolf targets identified by the Seer. 
*   Villagers: Execute established strategy – overwhelming votes based on direct evidence, minimizing debate. Counter ‘wolf pack’ pile-ons with demand for specific, verifiable evidence. Monitor attempts to deflect suspicion (changing subject, attacking Seer’s methods).
*   Guard: Only protect the Witch if the Seer specifically requests it.
