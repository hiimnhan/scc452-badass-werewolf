KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Seer's Timing: Claiming a 'confirmed' innocent too early created a narrative of collusion that the wolves exploited. 2. Guard's Target Error: Protecting a wolf (Meg) instead of the Seer/Witch neutralized the Guard's value. 3. Witch's Resource Mismanagement: Using the poison on a 'disruptive' player rather than a logically proven wolf. 4. Tone-Based Analysis: Villagers focused on whether Hoang sounded 'defensive' rather than tracking the fact that the Seer was exiled and the Guard was protecting enemies.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: Avoid immediate 'confirmation' of players on Day 1. This often looks like 'wolf-shielding' and makes you an easy target for wolves to frame as a fake. Instead, provide a lead that requires the village to verify through behavior. Guard: Your primary objective is to protect the Seer or Witch. Protecting a suspected wolf (Meg) is a critical waste of utility and actively assists the wolves. Witch: Your poison is your most powerful tool; never use it based on 'disruptive influence' or personality. Only poison players who have been contradicted by the Seer or shown a mathematical inconsistency in their claims. Villagers: Stop analyzing 'tone' (e.g., 'defensiveness' or 'aggressiveness'). Focus on role logic and voting blocks. In Round 1, the wolves led a consensus to exile the Seer; when you see a 'pile-on' against a power role, pause and question if you are being led by a wolf-driven narrative. Analyze who consistently votes with the wolves to identify the remaining threats.
