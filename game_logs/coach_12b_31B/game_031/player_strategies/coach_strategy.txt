• The 'Observer' Execution: Players who vote 'None' or claim to be 'observing' for two consecutive rounds are primary targets. Neutrality in the face of conflict is a wolf's greatest shield; force them to commit or exile them.
• Seer Sanctuary: The Seer's 'Not a Wolf' result is the only objective truth. Any player attacking a cleared player or the Seer is an immediate suspect. The village must treat the Seer as the highest priority asset.
• Guard's Sacred Duty: The Guard must protect the Seer every single night. Protecting a random villager while the Seer is alive is a strategic failure.
• Witch's Poison Discipline: Poison is for confirmed wolves or consensus kills only. Using poison based on 'vibes' or 'aggression'—especially against a claimed Seer—is an automatic loss condition.
• The Third-Party Pivot: In a 'He-said/She-said' deadlock (e.g., Nhan vs. Hoang), the village must stop focusing on the combatants and instead target the 'neutral' players who are avoiding the conflict.
• Anti-Chaos Logic: Be skeptical of players who describe the debate as 'too noisy,' 'frantic,' or 'premature.' This language is used to justify inaction and let the clock run out on the villagers.
• Role-Claim Skepticism: While transparency is good, be wary of players who demand immediate role claims to create a sense of urgency or to 'flush out' others; this can be a tool for wolves to map the village's power roles.
