KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Witch's Fatal Error: Poisoning the Seer is the single greatest mistake possible for the villager side. It deleted the objective truth from the game.
2. Misinterpreting the 'None' Vote: The Werewolves abstained twice, using pseudo-intellectual language to appear neutral. The village ignored this signal, focusing instead on arguing with each other.
3. Failure to Protect the Seer's Logic: The village allowed the debate to shift toward 'suspicious behavior' and 'deflection' rather than sticking to the empirical fact that the Seer had cleared a player. This allowed the Wolves to gaslight the village into exiling fellow villagers.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop relying on 'behavioral vibes' and start using Seer data as the only objective truth. When the Seer clears a player, anyone attacking that player is a high-probability Wolf. In Round 1, the focus shifted to 'verification' and 'motives' rather than acting on the clear result. Furthermore, treat 'None' votes as a massive red flag. The Werewolves used 'intellectual' language to encourage hesitation; any player refusing to commit during a divide is likely a Wolf trying to stay invisible.

Seer: You provided a clear result, but failed to explicitly claim your role early. This gave Wolves an opening to frame your behavior as 'irregular.' Declare your role immediately to anchor the village's logic.

Guard: You correctly protected the Seer in Round 2, but the village failed to coordinate around the Seer's survival. Once a Seer is protected, their next investigation result must be treated as law.

Witch: This was a catastrophic failure of role protocol. Never use a poison potion on a claimed Seer. By poisoning the Seer, you eliminated the village's only source of truth and handed the victory to the Wolves. Your priority is to protect the Seer and only poison players who have been confirmed as Wolves by the Seer.
