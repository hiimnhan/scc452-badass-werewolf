• Seer-Centric Logic: The Seer's data is the village's only objective truth. Any player attacking the Seer or a player the Seer has cleared is a high-probability Wolf. Prioritize 'what' was discovered over 'how' it was said.
• The Abstention Red Flag: Voting 'None' or abstaining is a primary Wolf signal. Wolves benefit from inaction. Treat refusal to commit during a clear conflict as a sign of guilt.
• Power Role Synergy: The Guard must protect the Seer every night. The Witch must save the Seer and use poison only on those who attack the Seer or cleared villagers.
• Concrete Behavioral Markers: Replace vague terms like 'suspicious' with specific markers: 'Player X attacked a cleared villager' or 'Player X abstained while others committed.'
• The 'Aggression' Fallacy: Wolves often frame a Seer's urgency as 'manipulation.' Do not let psychological framing override the Seer's data.
• Dead-Man's Truth: When a claimed Seer dies, the village must treat their last known investigation as a hard fact. Any player who attempts to cast doubt on a dead Seer's claim is likely a Wolf.
• Anti-Circular Logic: When two villagers attack each other (e.g., Cong vs Jin), the village must look for the 'Third Party' who is steering the chaos or abstaining to avoid the crossfire, rather than picking a side in the fight.
