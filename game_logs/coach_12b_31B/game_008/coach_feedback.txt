KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Seer Exile: The village failed to protect the Seer, who provided a confirmed innocent (Guard) in Round 1. Exiling the Seer is a catastrophic failure. 
2. Relying on 'Vibes': Players spent the entire game arguing about 'deflection' and 'patterns' rather than tracking Seer results and voting records. This allowed the remaining Wolf (Riley) to hide in plain sight by simply agreeing with the dominant narrative. 
3. Lack of Power Role Synergy: The Guard and Witch did not coordinate to ensure the Seer's survival. 
4. Failure to Identify the 'Agreement Trap': The village fell for the Wolf's strategy of validating other players' suspicions to steer the vote toward the Seer.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Stop using 'behavioral analysis' (e.g., calling something a 'classic wolf tactic' or 'deflection'). These psychological buzzwords create noise and allow Wolves to blend in. Rely exclusively on hard evidence. 

Seer: You must be the village's North Star. When you confirmed the Guard as innocent in Round 1, that was an absolute fact. Any player attacking a Seer-confirmed innocent is a high-probability Wolf. 

Guard/Witch: Your priority is the Seer. The Guard should protect the Seer once their identity is known, and the Witch must save the Seer at all costs. In this game, the Seer was left unprotected and exiled, which effectively ended the village's chance of winning. 

Villagers: Beware the 'Agreement Trap.' When players rapidly validate each other's 'vibes' without citing game facts, they are likely simulating consensus. Instead of agreeing that someone 'sounds' suspicious, ask: 'What is the evidence?' 

Vote-Tracking: Shift focus from how people speak to how they vote. When the Seer is exiled, look at who pushed for that vote—the remaining Wolf is almost certainly among them. If a debate devolves into mutual accusations of 'tactics,' stop the conversation and demand a list of known innocents based on Seer data.
