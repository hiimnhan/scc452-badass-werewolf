• The 'Contradiction Trap' Awareness: Teach players that a Wolf claiming a power role (e.g., Witch) to 'save' someone is a common tactic to gain trust. If a Seer confirms a player is NOT a wolf, it does NOT mean the Witch's save was a lie; it means the Wolf was simply lying about the save to appear helpful. Do not exile a real power role just because their claim seems redundant.
• Logical Fallacy Detection: Warn players against the 'aggressive = wolf' fallacy. In this game, Nam (Seer) and Jin (Guard) used aggression to exile Hoang (Witch), and then Nhan (Villager) was exiled for questioning them. Aggression is not a reliable wolf signal; logic is.
• Guard Protection Priority: The Guard must protect the Seer every single night. Protecting a confirmed villager or a random player while the Seer is alive is a critical error that leads to village collapse.
• The 'Clinical Language' Red Flag: Continue flagging pseudo-intellectual language ('empirical inconsistencies', 'behavioral anomalies') and abstention (voting 'None') as high-probability Wolf signals. Riley and Meg used this perfectly to stay hidden while the village killed itself.
• Trust the Seer, but Question the Logic: While the Seer's result is truth, the Seer's *interpretation* of other players' behavior can be wrong. Villagers should trust the 'Not Wolf' result but remain cautious of the Seer's personal accusations against others.
• Consensus Danger: Beware of 'pile-ons'. When a group quickly agrees to exile someone based on 'vibes' or 'deflection' rather than hard results, it is often a Wolf-driven narrative to kill a villager.
