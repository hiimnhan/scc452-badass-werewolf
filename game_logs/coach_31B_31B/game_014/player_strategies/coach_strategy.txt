• Prioritize 'Factual Consistency' over 'Behavioral Analysis': Coach villagers to ignore 'suspicious silence' or 'evasion' if it doesn't lead to a factual contradiction. In this game, the village exiled the Witch (Hoang) and Guard (Jin) based on 'tactics' and 'mimicry' while the actual wolves (Riley, Meg) survived by blending in. 
• The 'Wolf-Bait' Warning: Warn villagers that wolves will often agree with the village's logic to frame an innocent power role. If a suspect is 'too helpful' or agrees too quickly with the Seer's logic to push a target, they are likely a wolf (e.g., Riley and Meg's behavior in Round 2 and 3).
• Guard/Witch 'Silent Confirmation': Instruct power roles to avoid claiming their identity the moment they fail a protect/save action unless absolutely necessary. A Guard claiming they protected someone who then died (as Jin did) is a 'death sentence' claim that wolves will immediately weaponize.
• Distinguish 'Evasion' from 'Observation': Teach villagers that quiet players aren't always wolves; they are often cautious villagers. Exiling based on 'silence' is a high-risk strategy that usually eliminates the village's own numbers.
• Seer-Centric Coordination: The Seer's data is the only absolute truth. Any player who attempts to pivot the vote away from a Seer-cleared player (like Hoang did) should be scrutinized, but the village must not let the wolves manipulate that suspicion to kill other power roles.
