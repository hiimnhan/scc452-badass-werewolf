KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Behavioral Bias: The village exiled the Witch and Guard based on 'tactics,' 'mimicry,' and 'silence' rather than factual evidence, allowing the actual wolves to survive by blending in.
2. The Guard's Fatal Claim: The Guard claimed to have protected the Seer after the Seer had already died, providing the wolves with a factual inconsistency to use as a weapon.
3. The Witch's Tactical Error: The Witch attempted to force a vote on a Seer-cleared player to avoid a deadlock, which created a false signal of wolf behavior and led to their own exile.
4. Failure to Spot Wolf Mimicry: The village failed to notice that the wolves were simply agreeing with the majority's logic to frame innocent power roles, treating their agreement as 'help' rather than a deception tactic.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:

Seer: Your data is the only absolute truth. Continue providing clear leads, but be aware that wolves will aggressively agree with you to blend in. Anyone who pivots to support your logic only after a target is established (like the wolves did in Round 2) should be your primary suspects.

Guard: Never claim your role the moment a protection fails. Claiming you protected someone who then died is a 'death sentence' claim that wolves will immediately weaponize to frame you as a fake. Stay hidden until your claim can provide a factual benefit.

Witch: Be extremely careful when pushing for an exile to 'break a deadlock.' Targeting a player who was cleared by the Seer—even as a tactical move—makes you look like a werewolf. Your actions must align perfectly with the Seer's data to maintain trust.

General Voting: Be wary of 'Wolf-Bait.' When the wolves (Riley/Meg) agree with the village's logic to push a target, they are not helping; they are manipulating. If a player is 'too helpful' or agrees too quickly with the majority to exile a power role, scrutinize them.
