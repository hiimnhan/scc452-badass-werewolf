KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Potion Misuse: The Witch poisoned the Guard during a role dispute, effectively doing the wolves' work for them and removing a protective role.
2. Logic vs. Tone: The village exiled Nam—the only player focusing on factual inconsistencies—because his tone was perceived as 'aggressive' or 'questioning,' while the wolves successfully mimicked the Seer's authority to appear loyal.
3. Failure to Resolve Hard Contradictions: The village ignored the paradox of both the Guard and Witch claiming to save the same person. This should have been the central focus of the debate, rather than attacking 'stalling' players.
4. Over-Reliance on Seer: The Seer and the village failed to realize that a 'cleared' player making logically impossible claims is a signal to re-evaluate the entire narrative.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop equating 'confidence' or 'decisiveness' with innocence. Nam correctly identified a factual contradiction in the Witch's claim, but the village prioritized the tone of the debate over game logic. When a player's claim contradicts the game state (e.g., claiming a save when no one died), treat it as a primary red flag regardless of how 'stalling' others seem.

Seer: Your clearances are powerful, but you must evaluate the logical consistency of the people you clear. When the Witch and Guard entered a hard contradiction, you blindly trusted the Witch because of your check, ignoring that the Witch's claim about the save was illogical. A cleared player can still be wrong or confused; don't let a clearance blind you to factual lies.

Guard: You were an easy target because you entered a direct conflict with the Witch. When a contradiction occurs, instead of aggressively attacking, present the timeline of your action clearly. Your death removed a critical layer of protection for the Seer.

Witch: You committed a fatal error by using the Poison potion on the Guard simply because you were in a role contradiction. Never poison a fellow power-role claimant without external confirmation from the Seer that they are a wolf. By killing the Guard, you eliminated a villager ally and created a 'civil war' that the wolves exploited to frame Nam.

All: Be wary of 'Wolf-Shielding.' Riley and Meg used the Seer's authority as a shield, mirroring the Seer's logic to push for Nam's exile. If a player's only argument is 'The Seer said so,' they are often simulating compliance to hide their identity.
