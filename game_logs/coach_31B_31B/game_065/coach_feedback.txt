KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Exiling the Guard: The village prioritized 'tone policing' over the Guard's utility, removing a key defensive layer and leaving the Seer vulnerable.
2. Failure to Identify Social Mimicry: The wolves (Riley and Meg) successfully blended in by adopting the village's rhetoric and following the consensus, which the villagers mistook for alignment.
3. Power Role Disconnect: There was zero coordination between the Seer, Guard, and Witch. The Guard acted solo, and the Witch didn't verify the Guard's claim before the village voted him out.
4. Vibe-Based Voting: The transition from factual evidence (Seer's clear) to behavioral accusations (mirroring/vibes) allowed the wolves to manipulate the vote toward an innocent Villager in Round 2.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop prioritizing 'tone' or 'behavioral vibes' over role utility. You exiled your own Guard in Round 1 simply because he was aggressive. Abrasive behavior is not a wolf signal; a lack of factual contradiction is. Avoid the 'Consensus Trap'—Riley and Meg (Wolves) blended in by mirroring your vocabulary and voting with the majority. When a player like the Guard demands claims, evaluate if the request helps the village, not if the delivery is rude.

Seer: You provided a clear lead (Nhan), but you failed to coordinate your protection. You were killed in Round 2 because the Guard was already exiled. In future games, once you claim, your only priority is ensuring the Guard and Witch know exactly who you are to create a 'Power Core' of protection.

Guard: Your mistake was 'Urgency Bias.' Demanding immediate claims and framing hesitation as guilt often makes you look like a wolf trying to rush a vote. To be trusted, explain *why* the information is needed for the village's survival rather than attacking players' tones.

Witch: You effectively saved the target in Round 1, but you failed to cross-reference your actions with the Guard. If the Guard claims to have protected someone and they still die, you can identify the liar. In Round 2, you pivoted to 'vibe-based' accusations against the Villager, which played directly into the wolves' hands by creating internal friction.
