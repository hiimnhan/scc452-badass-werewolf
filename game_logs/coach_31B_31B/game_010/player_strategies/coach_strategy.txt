• Combat 'The Logic Trap': Warn villagers that wolves often use 'mechanical contradictions' as a weapon to frame power roles. If a player claims a mechanical error occurred, villagers must verify if that 'error' is actually a known role ability (e.g., the Witch's save) before trusting the accuser.
• Implement 'The Rule of Three' for Power Roles: Teach the village to cross-reference claims. If a Seer and Witch both claim to have interacted with the same target, the village should treat the interaction as a data point to be verified, not an immediate contradiction to be punished.
• Prevent 'The Consensus Cascade': Alert players when the entire group pivots to a single target based on the 'logic' of one dominant voice. Encourage a 'Devil's Advocate' approach where at least one player questions the mechanical premise of an accusation.
• Guard Against 'The Enforcer Trap': Warn against trusting players who aggressively 'police' the conversation or label others as 'textbook wolves.' This aggressive shielding of the narrative is often a wolf tactic to isolate skeptics.
• Prioritize Survival over Certainty: Coach Guards and Witches to prioritize the Seer's survival over trying to 'prove' their own innocence through debate. If a power role is under attack, the priority is protection, not winning the argument.
