KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Falling for Pseudo-Logic: The village allowed the wolves to frame the Witch as 'aggressive' and 'baseless' simply because they were the only ones pushing for action. The wolves successfully shifted the conversation from 'Who is the wolf?' to 'Is Nam's tone appropriate?' 2. Failure of Power Role Coordination: The Witch did not save any of the early casualties, leaving the village blind and without a Seer. 3. The Consensus Cascade: The Villager and the rest of the group followed a narrative of 'waiting for data' that was designed by the wolves to paralyze the village. By voting for the only person attempting to find the wolves, the village effectively purged its own active defense.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: You were eliminated too early to provide data. In future games, if you survive the first night, consider a 'soft claim' or providing a vague hint to the village to prevent them from spiraling into baseless meta-arguments. Guard: Your protection priority was inefficient; protecting yourself on Night 1 is a gamble. Prioritize protecting the Seer or the Witch to preserve the village's mechanical advantage. Witch: You were too passive. You failed to use a healing potion to save the Seer or Guard, and you didn't use a poison potion to test a suspicious player. When the debate became a clash of 'narratives,' you should have used your role's authority to steer the village toward a decision rather than letting the wolves dictate the logic. Villagers: Beware of 'Intellectual Framing.' The wolves used high-level vocabulary like 'mechanical contradiction' and 'narrative steer' to sound logical while providing zero actual data. When a player spends more time critiquing the process of the debate than identifying the wolf, they are likely using a pseudo-intellectual shield to mask their identity. Do not be fooled by a 'patient' approach that advocates for 'None' votes; this often serves the wolves by letting the clock run out on the villagers.
