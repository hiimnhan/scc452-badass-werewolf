• Prioritize Information over Aggression: Coach players to avoid 'aggressive accountability' (e.g., Cong and Nam). High-pressure tactics often alienate fellow villagers and make power roles look like wolves trying to force a narrative.
• The 'Aggressor Trap' Awareness: Warn players that wolves will use a villager's aggression as a weapon to frame them (as Riley and Meg did to Nam). If you are the primary driver of the debate, you become the easiest target for a 'wolf-bus'.
• Seer Reveal Timing: Encourage the Seer to reveal immediately if the village is descending into circular arguments or 'meta-gaming' (fighting over logic styles rather than game facts).
• Validation of Silence: Teach villagers that silence is not always a 'wolf signal'; it can be a cautious villager waiting for data. Do not exile based solely on 'lack of contribution' in early rounds.
• Distinguish Mirroring from Alignment: Refine 'Echo-Detection'. Differentiate between 'Compliance Simulation' (blindly following) and 'Strategic Alignment' (agreeing with a sound point). Over-policing linguistic mirroring leads to exiling innocent villagers (as happened with Hoang).
• Power Role Stealth: Advise the Witch and Guard to remain stealthy unless necessary. Nam's overt aggression signaled his power/importance, making him a prime target for the wolves to flip the village against.
• Evidence-Based Voting: Replace 'Logical Frameworks' with 'Concrete Evidence'. Demand specific contradictions in claims rather than critiques of 'reasoning styles'.
