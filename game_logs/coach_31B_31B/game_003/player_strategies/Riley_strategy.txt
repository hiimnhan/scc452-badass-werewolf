• Adaptive Aggression: Avoid early, high-profile conflicts with power-claimers (Seers) if the village is already leaning toward them; instead, plant seeds of doubt through questioning rather than direct attacks to avoid being the primary target.
• The 'Third-Party' Pivot: Instead of just following the majority, position yourself as the 'logical mediator' who weighs two conflicting arguments. This mimics villager utility without requiring a power claim.
• Calculated Betrayal: Use 'wolf-on-wolf' or 'wolf-on-suspect' votes strategically. Meg's vote against Nhan in Round 2 was crucial for blending in by aligning with the logic of the villagers (Nam/Cong) rather than blindly protecting a fellow wolf or staying silent.
• Weaponize Rule Technicalities: Identify and amplify slips in other players' logic (e.g., Nhan's 'private knowledge' slip). This makes you appear observant and dedicated to the village's victory.
• Target the 'Logic-Driven' Villager: Focus on players who demand 'hard evidence' (like Nam). Frame their request for proof as a 'delay tactic' or 'smokescreen' to turn the village against them.
• Controlled Visibility: Maintain a consistent presence in debate. Avoid the 'Passenger' label by asking targeted questions to others, which shifts the spotlight away from you while keeping you engaged.
