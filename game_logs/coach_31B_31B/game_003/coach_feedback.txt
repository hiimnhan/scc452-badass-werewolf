KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Over-reliance on behavioral tropes: The village frequently used phrases like 'classic wolf tactic' and 'textbook behavior.' While successful here, this is a logical fallacy; wolves can mimic these behaviors, and villagers can act 'suspiciously.' Always prioritize factual contradictions over 'vibes.'
2. Power Role Coordination: The Witch and Seer's synchronized rhetoric created a 'coordinated narrative' that the wolves almost successfully used to sow doubt. Power roles must support each other without appearing as a monolithic bloc.
3. Guard Passivity: The Guard's failure to engage in the day debate allowed the wolf to pivot the target toward them, creating unnecessary chaos and distraction from the actual wolf.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: Your results were accurate, but your communication style was overly aggressive. By demanding 'logical contradictions' and labeling dissent as 'textbook wolf behavior,' you inadvertently provided the wolves with a narrative to frame you as a fake Seer using intimidation. In future games, present your data as the foundation and let the village reach the conclusion; avoid silencing dissent, as it makes the village look coordinated and suspicious. 

Witch: You provided critical support by saving the Seer, but your daytime behavior was too aligned with the Seer. By echoing the Seer's phrasing and calling the wolf's arguments 'behavioral rushes,' you appeared to be 'shielding' them. To avoid being framed as a fake-coordinated pair, support the data but use your own distinct logical path to arrive at the suspect. 

Guard: You were highly effective in your night actions, protecting the Seer exactly when needed. However, your daytime passivity was a liability. By remaining silent, you became a target for the wolf's 'smokescreen' narrative. Even if you agree with the Seer, you must contribute your own analysis to the debate to prove your utility to the village. 

Villagers: You correctly prioritized objective data over behavioral tropes. However, be cautious of 'benefit analysis.' When a player like the second wolf switches to voting for their own teammate, analyze if they are genuinely convinced or simply trying to blend in. Ensure you are mapping a logical path to guilt rather than just following the Seer's lead.
