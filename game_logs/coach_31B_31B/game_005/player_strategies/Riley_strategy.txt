• Deferred Confrontation: Accept early Seer clears to build trust, but pivot to questioning the 'Enforcer'—the player most aggressively pushing the Seer's narrative—to frame them as controlling the village's reasoning.
• Tactical Synthesis: Use mediator language ('If X is true, then Y') to appear as the objective analyst. This masks your alignment while steering the vote toward targets who are logically isolated.
• Controlled Betrayal: Be prepared to vote for a fellow wolf if they become a lightning rod for suspicion. This establishes an 'irrefutable' villager persona that can carry the late game.
• Rule-Based Accusations: Focus attacks on factual contradictions or game-mechanic errors (e.g., claiming knowledge of dead roles). These are harder to defend than behavioral arguments and appear more objective to the village.
• Adaptive Invisibility: Balance participation. Being too silent makes you a target for 'utility' arguments; being too loud makes you a target for 'deflection' arguments. Aim to be the second or third person to confirm a majority direction.
