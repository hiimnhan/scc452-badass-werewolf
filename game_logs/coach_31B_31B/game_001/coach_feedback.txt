KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Seer's Logical Error: The Seer incorrectly speculated that a save occurred, which contradicted the actual game state. This allowed the village to exile their own Seer in Round 1 based on a perceived lie.
2. Confirmation Bias Loop: The Witch and Guard created a closed loop of trust. Because they verified each other, they stopped critically analyzing the remaining players, leading them to exile the innocent Villager (Jin) simply because he was passive and questioned their aggression.
3. Over-reliance on Behavioral Cues: The village repeatedly used phrases like 'textbook wolf tactic' to justify votes. They prioritized 'how someone spoke' over 'what evidence exists,' which is a dangerous habit that wolves can easily exploit by mimicking 'villager-like' passivity or 'leader-like' aggression.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
To the Seer: Your early mistake was speculating on a 'save' without confirmation. In Werewolf, guessing game states can be interpreted as a 'slip' by others. Stick to your investigation results; clearing a player is your strongest tool. Do not offer theories that contradict the actual night results, as this makes you an easy target for exile.

To the Guard and Witch: You formed a powerful 'power-role bubble' that successfully identified the wolves but created a dangerous confirmation bias. While your coordination was excellent, you began labeling any dissent or passivity as 'textbook wolf behavior.' This led to the accidental exile of an innocent villager. Use your shared knowledge to clear players, but do not use it to bulldoze those who are simply cautious.

To the Villagers: Avoid the trap of 'silent guilt.' Being passive is not evidence of being a wolf. When you are a plain villager, your goal is to provide a logical check on the power roles. If the Witch and Guard are rushing a vote, ask for the specific evidence that proves the suspect is a wolf, rather than accepting 'behavioral' arguments. When bidding for speech, use your turn to test alternative hypotheses—ask, 'If this person is a villager, why would they act this way?' to prevent the village from blindly following a dominant narrative.
