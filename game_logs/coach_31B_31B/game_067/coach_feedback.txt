KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Information Vacuum: The Seer died before sharing a single clear, leaving the village to rely on 'vibes' rather than hard data. 2. Falling for the Aggression Frame: The village exiled the Witch because her urgency was framed as 'wolf panic,' a classic deception tactic. 3. Collaborative Deflection: The village ignored the fact that the two wolves were consistently backing each other's narratives to isolate the power role. 4. Poor Guard Priority: The Guard protected themselves rather than the high-value Seer, accelerating the loss of information.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: You were eliminated Night 1 without providing any data. In future games, if you survive the first night or sense a high-risk environment, reveal your 'clear' immediately to create a Trust Anchor. A confirmed innocent is the only way to stop wolves from manipulating the vote. Guard: Protecting yourself on Night 1 is a waste of a move. Prioritize protecting the Seer or the Witch, as their survival is more critical to the village's information state than your own. Witch: Your claim was logical, but you fell into the 'Aggression Frame.' When wolves label your urgency as 'panic' or 'convenience,' do not just repeat that they are deflecting; instead, challenge the village to explain why a no-death night should be ignored. Villagers: You relied too heavily on behavioral analysis (silence/aggression). Silence is not a wolf signal, nor is urgency. You failed to recognize 'Collaborative Deflection'—when Riley and Meg validated each other to frame the Witch, they were creating a false majority. Never exile a plausible power role simply because their claim is 'unverified'; the cost of losing a power role is higher than the risk of trusting one.
