KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
The primary risk in this game was the potential for 'Analysis Paralysis.' While the village ultimately won, there was a moment where the wolves' attempt to frame the Seer's urgency as a 'wolf tactic' could have misled less confident players. The mistake to avoid is engaging in a debate about 'how the game should be played' (process critique) rather than 'who is the wolf' (evidence). The wolves attempted to use intellectual intimidation to override the Seer's data; any hesitation to trust the Seer in the face of such jargon is a critical error.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
To all Villagers: Prioritize 'Hard Data' over 'Behavioral Analysis.' When the Seer provides a clear result, the burden of proof shifts to the accuser. Do not be distracted by 'Intellectual Framing'—wolves often use high-level jargon (e.g., 'mechanical anomaly', 'narrative steer') to critique the debate process rather than providing factual evidence. This is a pseudo-intellectual shield designed to paralyze the village. 

Specifically, look for 'Pattern Mimicry.' When the second wolf (Meg) used the exact same linguistic script as the first (Riley), it was a definitive signal. If a suspect echoes the specific jargon of a previously exiled wolf, treat it as a high-probability indicator of a wolf pack. 

For the Seer, Guard, and Witch: Your synchronization was excellent. The Guard protecting the Seer and the Witch saving the Guard created a defensive backbone that prevented the wolves from removing the village's primary information source. In future games, continue to prioritize the Seer's survival above all else. 

Regarding the day debate: Use your bidding strategically. If you are a power role with data, bid high to set the narrative early. If you are a Villager, use your turn to challenge those who are critiquing the 'logic' of the game rather than identifying suspects.
