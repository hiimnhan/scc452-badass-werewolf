• Strategic Sacrifice: Use a 'Pawn' to execute a high-visibility attack on the most aggressive villager (the Enforcer) when a power role claims. This draws heat away from the 'Survivor' and creates permanent doubt about the Enforcer's alignment.
• The 'Mechanical' Mask: Adopt the vocabulary of the opposing side. Use terms like 'mechanical contradiction,' 'narrative steering,' and 'factual evidence' to frame accusations. This makes wolf-logic indistinguishable from villager-logic.
• Voting Divergence: In Round 1, the Survivor must never vote with the Pawn. Vote for the Pawn or a neutral party to erase any perceived wolf-bloc coordination.
• The Skeptic's Pivot: Once the Pawn is exiled, the Survivor must transition to a 'Cautious Observer.' Avoid direct aggression; instead, question the 'convenience' of the power role's claims to sow internal friction among villagers.
• Shield Framing: Identify the player most vocal in defending power roles and frame them as the 'Mechanical Shield'—the actual lead wolf using the power role as a tool to purge dissenters.
• Calculated Silence: Maintain a low profile in early rounds to avoid becoming the primary target, then strike with 'logical' analysis once the village is divided.
