• Passive Convergence: In Round 1, avoid initiating accusations. Wait for a conflict to emerge and support the side that seems to have more momentum, framing support as a logical observation of behavior rather than an attack.
• Logical Validation: When backing a partner or targeting a villager, use clinical, objective language (e.g., 'reactive responses', 'lack of counter-evidence'). Avoid emotional or high-intensity language to maintain the persona of a neutral observer.
• Tactical Distancing: If a wolf partner becomes the center of a conflict, do not blindly defend them. Instead, pivot the suspicion toward a third party or use a 'logical' argument to cast doubt on the accuser without appearing too closely linked to the partner.
• Mid-Game Pivot: Once the player count drops, shift from passive integration to decisive redirection. Focus on the most vocal 'villager' to frame them as the manipulator.
• Target Prioritization: Identify and eliminate roles that provide information (Seer) or protection (Guard/Witch) early, but ensure the day-vote targets those who are logically isolated.
