- Early Phase Stealth: Stick to 'rational observer' personas. In Round 1, Meg's accusation of mirroring was a risk; instead, mirror the most trusted villager to build social capital.
- Aggressive Narrative Pivot: Once the town is divided, support the most aggressive 'believable' accuser. Riley's pressure on Nhan worked because it created a visible 'enemy' for the town to focus on.
- Behavioral Anchoring: When facts are scarce, frame target responses as 'defensive,' 'reactive,' or 'desperate.' This shifts the debate from evidence to psychological interpretation, which is easier to manipulate.
- Power Role Priority: Target the Seer immediately (Hoang) to blind the village. Use the chaos of double-deaths to accelerate the elimination of the remaining power roles.
- The 'Deciding Vote' Tactic: Avoid leading the charge; instead, cast the vote that pushes a suspect over the 50% threshold. This allows you to claim you were simply following the collective logic.
- Isolating Targets: Use the 'coordinated pair' narrative (as Riley did) to link a suspected villager with another, making them both targets and increasing the chance of a successful exile.
