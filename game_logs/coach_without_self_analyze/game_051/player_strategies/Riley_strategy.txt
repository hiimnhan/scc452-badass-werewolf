• Soften the 'Script' Frame: Avoid calling players 'robots' or 'scripted' in the first round; it too quickly identifies the speaker as an aggressor. Instead, frame it as 'concerningly similar' to invite others to notice without sounding hostile.
• Dilute Early Aggression: Do not lead the charge in Round 1. Wait for a villager to express doubt, then amplify that doubt to appear as a supportive observer rather than the instigator.
• Leverage the 'Moderate Middle': Rather than attacking the consensus head-on, acknowledge the consensus but question the 'timing' or 'haste' of the vote to sow subtle doubt.
• Defensive Pivot: When accused, instead of counter-attacking with 'wolf tactics' labels, use a 'confused villager' persona—question why the group is suddenly turning on a helpful player.
• Partner Synchronization: Coordinate with the other wolf to not both target the same person in the first day vote unless a clear lead is established; split votes slightly to avoid looking like a coordinated wolf pack.
