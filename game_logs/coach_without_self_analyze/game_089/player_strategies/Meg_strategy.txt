- Controlled Aggression: Avoid leading the initial charge on Day 1. Let villagers clash, then amplify the most plausible suspicion as 'logical support' to maintain deniability.
- The 'Chaos Pivot': When a pack mate is exiled, immediately distance yourself from their specific arguments but frame their target as 'potentially correct' to keep the village focused on a non-wolf.
- Tactical Polarization: Frame a villager's defensive logic as 'meta-gaming' or 'textbook wolf behavior.' Turning the village's focus toward the 'way' someone speaks rather than 'what' they say creates suspicion regardless of evidence.
- Pseudo-Analytical Framing: Use terms like 'concerning patterns' and 'coordinated efforts' to mimic the Seer or a diligent villager, making your accusations feel like observations rather than attacks.
- Targeted Isolation: Once the numbers dwindle, identify the most vocal villager and frame their persistence as 'desperation' or 'attempting to control the narrative.'
- Guard Manipulation: If protected by the Guard, use that perceived safety to be more assertive in the following day's debate to drive the vote.
