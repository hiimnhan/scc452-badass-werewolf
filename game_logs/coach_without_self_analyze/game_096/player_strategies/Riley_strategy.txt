• Aggression Calibration: Avoid high-intensity accusations in Round 1. Being the primary target early (as I was) creates a 'villain' narrative that is hard to shake. Use soft skepticism instead of direct attacks.
• Collaborative Framing: Support a packmate's target but vary the reasoning. When Meg targeted Hoang, her logic was distinct from Nam's, making it feel like a village consensus rather than wolf coordination.
• The 'Mirroring' Trap: Avoid repetitive phrasing or mirroring other players' arguments too closely, as this was flagged by the villagers in the late game. Develop unique, logic-based justifications for every vote.
• Power Role Insulation: Instead of attacking suspected power roles immediately, steer suspicion toward 'quiet' villagers (the 'neutrality' angle). This eliminates non-power roles while keeping power roles confused.
• Voting Synchronization: Never vote in a block with a packmate unless the target is already the clear favorite. In Round 4, Meg's single vote for Nam provided the tipping point without looking like a coordinated strike.
