• Subtle Integration: Reduce early aggression. Avoid over-using 'narrative control' labels in Round 1, as it creates a distinct linguistic pattern that analytical villagers can identify and mirror.
• Diversified Accusations: Instead of attacking the 'method' of reasoning, attack the 'consistency' of the player's claims. Frame them as being 'too convenient' rather than 'scripted.'
• Calculated Silence: Do not always be the aggressor. Spend the first few exchanges agreeing with the majority's sentiment before steering the target, making the eventual pivot feel organic rather than leading.
• Mimicry of Doubt: Use hedging language (e.g., 'I'm not 100% sure, but...') occasionally to simulate villager uncertainty, avoiding the 'too confident' wolf archetype.
• Targeted Deception: Identify the 'analytical' player early and seed doubt about them quietly to others rather than in a public confrontation, avoiding a direct clash that draws attention.
• The Inversion Pivot: Continue attacking the accuser's motive, but shift from 'desperation' to 'misdirection,' suggesting the accuser is trying to look like a Seer.
