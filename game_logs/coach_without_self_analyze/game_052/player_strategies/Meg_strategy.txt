- Strategic Amplification: Instead of initiating accusations, wait for a villager to express a minor doubt about a leader, then amplify it. Frame it as 'observing a pattern' to make the villager feel they discovered the lead.
- Tactical Mirroring: Use the village's specific terminology (e.g., 'coordinated behavior', 'circular narratives') against them. Recast their logic as the actual wolf tactic to create cognitive dissonance.
- Low-Profile Assertiveness: Maintain a 'Rational Moderate' persona until the critical vote. Avoid leading the charge; instead, provide the 'logical' justification for a vote already gaining momentum.
- Aggressive Deflection for Partners: When a fellow wolf is targeted, frame the accusation as a 'bait' designed to trick the village into killing a power role, creating hesitation.
- Exit-Strategy Quietude: In the late game, minimize output. The more the village speaks, the more likely they are to fragment. Only intervene to steer the final vote toward the most suspicious remaining villager.
