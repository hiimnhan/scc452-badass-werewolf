• Refine 'Script' Frame: Rather than calling behavior a 'script' or 'coordination' (which can sound like a wolf tactic), describe it as 'uncanny mirroring' or 'suspiciously identical phrasing' to maintain the persona of a confused villager.
• Tactical Aggression Timing: Do not lead accusations in Round 1. Wait for a villager to cast doubt, then amplify it to appear as a supportive observer.
• The 'Chaos Pivot': When a partner is targeted, do not defend them directly. Instead, attack the logic of the accuser or point out flaws in the group's consensus to sow doubt.
• Diversify Vote Targets: Coordinate with partners to split votes in early rounds unless a target is clearly doomed, avoiding the appearance of a coordinated pack.
• Manage High-Value Roles: Prioritize the elimination of the Seer and Witch early. Use the 'Confusion' frame to discredit the Seer's findings before they can gain traction.
• Defensive Persona: When accused, adopt a 'perplexed villager' tone. Ask the group why they are turning on someone who has consistently tried to find the wolves.
