• Mechanical Priority: A Seer's 'Clear' is the most powerful tool. If a Seer clears Player A, and Player B claims a mechanical action (like a Witch save) that they argue 'contradicts' that clear, the village must recognize that a save does NOT negate a clear. A player can be both saved by a Witch AND be a non-wolf. Anyone claiming these are contradictory is likely a Wolf attempting to gaslight the village.
• The 'Save' Logic Trap: Coach Witch players to understand that their save is a confirmation of a target, not a contradiction of a Seer's investigation. If a Witch claims a Seer is lying because they saved the same person, the Witch is either fundamentally confused or a Wolf.
• Guard/Witch Information Silos: Encourage power roles to share their actions only when it provides a mechanical proof of innocence. In this game, the Witch's claim was used as a weapon to kill the Seer; coach players to ask, 'Does this information actually prove the other person is a wolf, or is it just creating noise?'
• Trust the Seer Over the 'Save': In a conflict between a Seer's 'Clear' and a Witch's 'Save' (where the Witch claims the Seer is lying), the Seer is mathematically more likely to be truthful. The 'Save' provides no information about the target's role, only that they were attacked.
• Identify Wolf-Driven Consensus: Watch for 'pile-on' voting. When a confused villager (like Nhan) starts a narrative, Wolves (like Riley and Meg) will pivot to support that narrative to ensure the power role (Seer) is exiled. Coach players to question the 'consensus' when it targets the Seer.
