KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Mechanical Illiteracy: The Witch and Villagers believed a Seer should 'protect' targeted players, confusing the Seer's role with the Guard's. This led to the execution of the Witch and the near-execution of the Seer. 2. The Verification Trap: The village prioritized a demand for 'proof' over the Seer's actual data, allowing the Wolves to frame the Seer as a deceiver. 3. Failure to Recognize Narrative Pivoting: The Wolves successfully used the Witch's confusion to create a false consensus, leading the village to exile their own power roles (Witch, then Villager) while the Wolves remained hidden by labeling others as 'agitators.' 4. Lack of Power Role Synergy: The Seer and Guard failed to coordinate their public narrative, with the Seer actually questioning the Guard's support, which signaled instability to the village.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop demanding 'irrefutable proof' from the Seer. A Seer's only proof is their findings and the survival of those they clear. Treating the Seer as a suspect because they lack 'verification' is a Wolf tactic that destroys your only source of truth. Do not confuse 'aggressive behavior' with 'wolf behavior'; exiling a disruptive player who is actually a power role is a catastrophic loss. 

Seer: You provided clear information, but you failed to build a 'trusted core.' Instead of questioning the Guard for supporting you, lean into that alliance. When the Guard protects you and you provide clears, you are the village's strongest asset—do not alienate your only protector. 

Guard: Your instincts were correct in supporting the Seer, but you must explain WHY. Instead of saying 'anyone attacking is a wolf,' explain that the Seer's utility outweighs the suspicion of their behavior. 

Witch: Your logic was fundamentally flawed. Being targeted by wolves does not mean the Seer is lying; these are independent actions. A Seer does not 'protect' people—the Guard does. By attacking the Seer for not protecting you, you led the village to execute its own power role. 

All: Watch for 'Echo-Voting.' When two players (like the wolves in this game) suddenly jump on a narrative started by a confused villager to 'pile on' a Seer, they are masking their identity. Prioritize mechanical facts (clears) over 'vibes' or 'behavioral patterns.'
