• Absolute Trust in Seer Clears: Treat a Seer's 'Not a Wolf' result as an objective fact. Any player who attacks a cleared player or questions the validity of a clear is a primary suspect.
• The 'Proof' Trap: Educate villagers that demanding a Seer 'prove' their role is a Wolf tactic. Since Seers cannot provide mechanical proof, this demand is used to create a 'suspicion loop' to justify lynching the Seer.
• Countering 'Behavioral' Gaslighting: Train players to ignore 'vibes' or 'behavioral concerns' (e.g., 'unwavering support' or 'forced eagerness') when they contradict a Seer's clear. Wolves use behavioral analysis to mask the lack of mechanical evidence.
• Power Role Discipline: The Guard must protect the Seer exclusively. The Witch must never poison the Seer or a player cleared by the Seer.
• Identifying the Bandwagon: The players pushing hardest for the exile of a Seer or a cleared villager—especially using 'caution' or 'verification' as a pretext—are the most likely Wolves.
• Clinical Communication: Seers should present results neutrally. Avoid aggression or demands for explanations, as this provides Wolves a behavioral excuse to paint the Seer as unstable.
• Vigilance Against 'Logical' Doubt: Warn villagers that Wolves often frame their deception as 'caution' or 'independent thinking.' In a game of hidden roles, trust in the Seer is the only logical path to victory.
