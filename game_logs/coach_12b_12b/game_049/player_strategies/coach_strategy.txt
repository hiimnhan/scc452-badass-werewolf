• Role Data Supremacy (Revised): A Seer's 'Clear' is the gold standard. Any player who attacks a cleared player or the Seer must be treated as a high-priority suspect. Behavioral 'vibes' must never override role data.
• The 'Self-Correction' Trap: Warn villagers that a Seer may occasionally make mistakes in social delivery, but the data remains true. Do not let Wolves use a Seer's 'aggressive' or 'flimsy' tone to invalidate a mechanical 'Clear'.
• Coordination Guardrails: The Guard and Witch must prioritize the Seer. The Witch must save the Seer over any other player. If the Seer is killed, the village loses its only objective compass.
• Anti-Psychology Filter: Explicitly train players to ignore terms like 'manufactured,' 'deflective,' 'unsettling,' or 'suspicious behavior.' Demand logical contradictions (e.g., 'Player A said X, but now says Y') instead of psychological assessments.
• The 'Echo' Detection: Identify players who mirror the majority's suspicion without providing new evidence. Wolves often 'blend in' by voting with the crowd against a villager they've successfully framed.
• Voting Logic Audit: Before voting, ask: 'Am I voting based on a role result or a feeling?' If it is a feeling, the vote should be withheld. Never vote against a cleared player unless a second, conflicting Seer claim emerges.
• Identifying 'The Frame': Recognize when Wolves create a narrative of 'fake coordination' to turn the village against the Seer and Guard. Agreement between the Seer and their cleared targets is a sign of success, not deception.
