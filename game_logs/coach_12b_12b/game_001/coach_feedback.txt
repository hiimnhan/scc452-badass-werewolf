KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Witch's Mechanical Failure: The Witch used both the save and poison potions on the same player, effectively killing them and then claiming a 'save' that didn't happen, creating a fake contradiction. 2. Logic Trap: Villagers relied on 'convenience logic' (e.g., 'this is too convenient') rather than mechanical verification, allowing the wolves to push a narrative against the Witch. 3. Failure to Protect the Seer: The village exiled the Witch and then allowed the wolves to kill the Seer immediately after, because they prioritized narrative conflict over the Seer's confirmed data. 4. Lack of Role Synergy: There was no coordination or understanding of how the Guard, Witch, and Seer actions intersect, leading to internal chaos that the wolves exploited.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
To the Witch: Your actions were mechanically contradictory. Saving and poisoning the same target (Nhan) ensures their death, making your claim of 'saving' them a falsehood in the eyes of the village. Never poison a target you intend to protect. To the Seer: You provided a confirmed 'Not Wolf' result early, which was correct, but you failed to explain how a Witch's save and a Seer's investigation can coexist. When challenged, explain the mechanics clearly to avoid appearing like a lying wolf. To the Guard: Your protection of the Witch was a waste of a turn given the Witch's self-sabotage. To the Villagers: Stop using 'convenience' as a metric for guilt. Riley and Meg (Wolves) successfully manipulated you by framing a mechanical misunderstanding as a 'convenient lie.' Instead of asking 'Who is lying?', ask 'How is this possible?' If a Seer provides a confirmed innocent, protect that information unless a mechanical impossibility is proven. In future debates, use your bidding power to prioritize the Seer's testimony over narrative suspicions.
