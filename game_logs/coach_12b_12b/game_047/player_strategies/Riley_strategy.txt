* **Early Game Observation (Cycles 1-3):** Absolute silence. Scrutinize voting patterns. Identify the most vocal accusers and defenders. Gauge paranoia levels—high paranoia is exploitable. 
* **Exploit Seer Claims:** If a Seer emerges, subtly question their methods, not their results. Cast doubt on their interpretations, planting seeds of uncertainty. 
* **Emotional Amplification:** Target players who react strongly to accusations. Provoke defensiveness, then leverage it against them.  
* **Adjacent Voting Priority:** Focus on those defending others—they are the linchpins. Eliminating them destabilizes the village’s trust network. 
* **'Mirror' Recognition & Exploitation:** If a player 'mirrors' our concerns (echoes our doubts), subtly guide them toward a false target. They become unwitting allies.
* **Meg as Asset:** Frame Meg's unusual behavior as insightful observation, not suspicion. ‘Meg sees what others miss’ is a consistent refrain. 
* **Fabrication Threshold:** Only fabricate accusations when paranoia is high and existing tensions are ripe for exploitation.  Base fabrications on existing, but tenuous, connections. 
* **Controlled Deflection (If Accused):** Respond with measured skepticism and a demand for concrete evidence. Echo villager interrogation tactics to deflect suspicion. 
* **Code Adaptation:** 'Mirror' still indicates an unwitting ally; 'Shift' signals a change in target. 'Anchor' denotes a player steadfastly defending another – high priority for elimination. 
* **Late-Game Chaos:** If the village is fractured, unleash a barrage of fabricated accusations to create maximum confusion and split votes. 
* **Avoid Direct Wolf Association:** Never explicitly defend another wolf. Let them fall if necessary to preserve your cover.
