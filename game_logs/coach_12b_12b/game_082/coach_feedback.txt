KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Power Role Sabotage: The Witch poisoned the Guard, and the Guard failed to protect the Seer, leading to a total collapse of the village's defensive structure.
2. The Proof Trap: The Villager and Witch fell for the 'demand for verification' fallacy, treating a Seer's clear as a suspicion rather than a fact.
3. Seer Communication: The Seer became emotionally aggressive, allowing Wolves to frame their behavior as 'suspicious' to the rest of the village.
4. Wolf-Mirroring: The Villagers failed to notice that the Wolves were the ones most aggressively pushing for the Seer's exile once the doubt was sown.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop prioritizing 'behavioral vibes' over mechanical clears. When the Seer clears a player, that player is a factually confirmed ally. Anyone demanding 'proof' of the Seer's role or questioning a clear—including other power roles—is likely a Wolf attempting to create a suspicion loop. In this game, the demand for verification was a trap that led to the Seer's exile.

Seer: Provide results clinically. Avoid aggressive rhetoric like 'blatant attempts' or 'desperate,' as this gives Wolves a behavioral excuse to paint you as erratic or manipulative. State the result and move on.

Guard: Your sole priority is the Seer. Protecting anyone else is a waste of resources. Once the Seer is identified, they must be protected every night until the game ends.

Witch: Never use your poison on a player cleared by the Seer. Poisoning a confirmed villager is a game-losing error that destroys the village's numerical advantage.

General Voting: When a Seer is under attack, look at who is pushing the hardest for the exile using 'behavioral concerns.' In this game, the Wolves mirrored the village's doubt to ensure the Seer was lynched. Trust the mechanical clear over the 'vibe' of the debate.
