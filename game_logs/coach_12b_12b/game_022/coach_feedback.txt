KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Behavioral Gaslighting: The village fell for the 'Vibe Trap,' exiling the Guard because his defense of the Seer felt 'too aggressive.' This allowed the wolves to eliminate a power role using fake villager logic.
2. Guard Misplacement: The Guard protected the Witch instead of the Seer, failing the most basic protection priority.
3. Resource Waste: The Witch used a SAVE potion on themselves rather than securing the Seer.
4. Ignoring the Anchor: The village exiled a player the Seer had explicitly cleared (Villager) in Round 2, simply because the cleared player was acting 'frantic.' Evidence must always override gut feelings.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop prioritizing 'vibes' over mechanical facts. When the Seer provides a 'Clear,' that player is an objective anchor. Do not exile players for being 'too forceful' or 'aggressive' when they are defending a confirmed fact; this is a classic wolf gaslighting tactic designed to make truth-tellers look suspicious. 

Seer: You provided clear information, but you failed to lead the village. When the village began doubting a confirmed clear, you should have explicitly labeled the skeptics as wolves. 

Guard: Your primary objective is the Seer. Protecting anyone else while the Seer is alive is a critical failure. By protecting the Witch instead of the Seer, you left the village's eyes vulnerable. 

Witch: You wasted your SAVE potion on yourself on Night 1. Save potions are high-value resources; they must be reserved for the Seer or the 'Trusted Core.' 

All: Once a Seer clears a player, those two must form an unbreakable voting block. Any player attempting to invalidate a Seer's result—even after the Seer's death—is a werewolf. In Round 2, the moment a cleared player (Villager) attacked the Seer, they should have been the primary target for exile.
