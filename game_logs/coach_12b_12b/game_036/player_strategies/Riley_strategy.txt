* **Prioritize Meg's Survival Above All:** Her presence is essential for victory. Shield her through coordinated votes and subtle redirection of suspicion. 
* **Early Game - Observation & Passive Reinforcement:** Remain silent for the first round. Observe voting patterns and identify key influencers (those readily swayed by others). Then, subtly reinforce the dominant narrative – don’t initiate, just amplify.
* **Leverage Existing Conflict – Amplify, Don’t Create:** Don’t manufacture doubt; capitalize on existing disagreements. Frame concerns as questions about logic, not accusations. “Why did X vote for Y *so* quickly?” is better than “X is suspicious.”
* **Adjacent Voting - Calculated Risk:** Continue, but refine. If a strong villager is targeted, vote for someone *slightly* less influential but still connected to them. Avoid voting for obvious villagers.
* **Seer Manipulation - Subtle Inconsistencies:** If the Seer reveals information, don’t challenge it directly. Instead, introduce minor inconsistencies: “That's interesting, but doesn't X's behavior *also* suggest…”
* **Deflection - Confidence & Targeted Questions:** If accused, immediately question the accuser’s motives and logic. Project absolute certainty. “Why are you so quick to accuse *me*? What are you hiding?”
* **Strategic Silence - The Power of Observation:** Significantly increase periods of silence. Only speak when a vote is imminent or suspicion is directly threatening Meg. 
* **Late-Game - Paranoia & Controlled Chaos:** As the numbers dwindle, subtly introduce doubt about *everyone*. Feed existing anxieties, but avoid direct accusations. The goal is to create a climate of distrust where villagers are too afraid to act decisively.
* **Wolf Sacrifice - Last Resort, Calculated:** Only sacrifice myself if exposure is unavoidable and it guarantees a key villager's elimination. Ensure Meg is protected.
