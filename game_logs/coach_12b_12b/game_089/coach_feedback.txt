KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Guard Failure: Protecting a Villager instead of the Seer in Round 1 left the Seer vulnerable. 2. Witch Catastrophe: Poisoning the Seer in Round 2 eliminated the village's only source of truth, guaranteeing a Wolf victory. 3. Logic Error: Villagers ignored the Seer's clear of Nhan, allowing Wolves to use 'suspicious coordination' rhetoric to frame innocent players. 4. Coordination Collapse: The power roles acted in isolation rather than as a synergistic unit to protect the Seer.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Prioritize 'Truth-Anchors.' When the Seer clears a player, that is objective data. Do not let behavioral arguments or 'vibes' override a Seer's clear. Anyone attacking a cleared player is likely a Wolf. 

Seer: Your information is the village's only weapon. Be more assertive in establishing your role early to ensure the Guard protects you. 

Guard: Your absolute priority is the Seer. Protecting a random Villager while the Seer is exposed is a critical failure. Once a Seer claims, they must be your primary target for protection to maintain the information flow. 

Witch: Use your poison with extreme discipline. Never poison a power role (Seer) who has provided clears and has no counter-claim. Poisoning the Seer is effectively helping the Werewolves win. 

All: Beware of 'Wolf Mirroring.' In this game, the Wolves used villager-like terminology ('deflection,' 'textbook tactics') to frame the Guard and Witch. Look at the source of information, not the style of the accusation.
