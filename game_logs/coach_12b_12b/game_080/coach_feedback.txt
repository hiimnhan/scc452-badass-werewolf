KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Witch's Logic Failure: The Witch mistakenly believed a 'Save' invalidated a Seer's clear, leading them to attack the Seer and create the initial seed of doubt. 2. Guard Misallocation: The Guard protected a Wolf (Meg) instead of the Seer, leaving the village vulnerable. 3. Failure to Recognize Wolf Rhetoric: The village ignored the Wolves' use of vague, behavioral descriptors ('erratic', 'unsettling') to frame the Seer, choosing 'vibes' over mechanical evidence. 4. Coordination Collapse: The power roles (Witch, Guard, Seer) worked against each other rather than forming a protective shield around the Seer's information.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
To the Seer: Your mechanical clears were accurate, but your tone became too aggressive. When you react with hostility to suspicion, you provide Wolves with the 'erratic' narrative they use to frame you. Stay clinical and focus on the logic of the clear, not the character of the accuser. To the Witch: This was a catastrophic misplay. Saving a player does not contradict a Seer's clear; it confirms the Wolves targeted that person. By attacking the Seer for clearing someone you saved, you actively dismantled the village's only source of truth and led the town to exile their own Seer. To the Guard: Your priority must always be the Seer. Protecting a random player (who turned out to be a Wolf) while the Seer is alive is a critical error in resource management. To the Villagers: You fell for 'momentum lynching.' You prioritized 'vibes' and behavioral buzzwords over a mechanical clear. When a Seer provides a 'Not Wolf' result, that player is a baseline of truth. Anyone attacking that baseline without evidence—like the Witch did—should be your primary suspect, not the Seer.
