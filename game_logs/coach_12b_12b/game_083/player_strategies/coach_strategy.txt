• Absolute Trust in Mechanical Clears: Treat a Seer's 'Not a Wolf' result as a hard fact. Any player (including other power roles) who attempts to 'verify' or 'question' a clear is a primary suspect. 
• The 'Proof' Trap: Explicitly coach villagers that demanding a Seer 'prove' their role is a classic Wolf tactic. Since Seers cannot provide mechanical proof in public, this demand is intended to create a 'suspicion loop' to justify lynching the Seer.
• Power Role Discipline: The Witch must never use their abilities to attack players cleared by the Seer. Poisoning a cleared player or the Seer is a game-losing error.
• Guard Logic: The Guard must protect the Seer exclusively until the Seer is dead or the game is won. Protecting others is a waste of a resource.
• Identifying 'Wolf-Mirroring': Train players to spot Wolves who 'join the bandwagon' against the Seer. In this game, Riley and Meg used 'behavioral' concerns to push for Jin's exile. When a Seer is being attacked, the people pushing the hardest for the exile are often the Wolves.
• Clinical Seer Communication: Encourage Seers to provide results without emotional baggage. Avoid aggressive rhetoric (e.g., calling others 'desperate') as this gives Wolves a 'behavioral' excuse to paint the Seer as erratic.
• Coordination over Intuition: Prioritize mechanical results (Seer clears) over 'vibes' or 'behavioral patterns'. If a player's behavior is 'suspicious' but they are cleared by the Seer, they are a villager; the person calling them suspicious is the target.
