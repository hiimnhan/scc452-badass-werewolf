KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. The Bandwagon Effect: The village fell for the 'Caution Trap.' The Wolves framed their attacks as 'independent thinking' and 'verification,' leading the Villagers and Witch to exile a cleared Villager and then the Seer himself.
2. Role Misalignment: The Guard failed to protect the Seer, and the Witch actively worked against the Seer's information during the day debate.
3. Ignoring Evidence: The village prioritized 'behavioral analysis' (questioning how a player supported the Seer) over the Seer's actual result, allowing the remaining Wolf to win by default.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop using 'behavioral concerns' to override Seer clears. When the Seer identifies a player as a Villager, any player attacking that clear (as the Wolves did by calling the Villager's support 'unsettling' or 'blind faith') is a primary suspect. Trust the Seer's mechanical result over 'vibes.'

Seer: Maintain clinical neutrality. Avoid demanding explanations or attacking others' behavior, as this gives Wolves a pretext to paint you as 'aggressive' or 'unstable.' Present your findings and let the village react.

Guard: Your priority is the Seer. Protecting a random Villager in Round 1 while the Seer is the Wolves' primary target is a critical error. The Seer is the only source of objective truth; they must be protected at all costs.

Witch: You successfully saved the Seer and poisoned a Wolf, but your Day debate was catastrophic. You spent your time questioning a Seer-cleared Villager and demanding 'validation' of the Seer. This is a Wolf tactic. Never sow doubt about a Seer's clear, especially when you have the insight of your own role.
