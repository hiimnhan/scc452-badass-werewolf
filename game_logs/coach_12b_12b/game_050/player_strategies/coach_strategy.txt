• Role Data Supremacy: A Seer's 'Clear' is absolute. Any attempt to frame a cleared player as 'too coordinated' with the Seer is a textbook Wolf tactic. Never vote against a cleared player unless a conflicting Seer claim is made.
• The 'Coordinated Defense' Fallacy: Explicitly train players to recognize that a Seer and their cleared target *should* agree. Wolves will attempt to label this 'mirroring' or 'suspicious coordination' to isolate the Seer. This is a frame, not a clue.
• Behavioral Noise Filter: Ignore psychological terms like 'mirroring,' 'too convenient,' or 'suspicious behavior.' Demand mechanical contradictions. If the only evidence is 'they sound the same,' it is noise.
• Guard/Witch Priority: The Seer is the village's only objective asset. The Guard must protect the Seer every single night unless the Seer is confirmed dead. The Witch must save the Seer over all others.
• The 'Wolf Blend' Audit: Identify players who shift their target to match the majority without adding new evidence. In this game, the Wolves (Riley/Meg) waited for Nam to create the 'coordination' narrative and then simply echoed it to seal the Seer's fate.
• Voting Logic Audit: Before voting, ask: 'Does this vote contradict a Seer clear?' If yes, the voter is likely a Wolf or a confused villager. A vote against a cleared player is a vote against the village.
