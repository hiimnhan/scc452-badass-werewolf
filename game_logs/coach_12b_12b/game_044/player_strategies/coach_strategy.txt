• The 'Hard-Clear' Absolute: A Seer's 'Not a Wolf' result is an objective fact. Any player who votes to exile a cleared player or the Seer is immediately flagged as a Wolf, regardless of the cleared player's behavior. Behavioral 'suspicion' is a Wolf's tool to override data.
• The 'Mechanical Justification' Trap: Coach players to ignore demands for 'how' a role works. In Werewolf, the role's result is the only justification needed. Demanding 'mechanical proof' is a common Wolf tactic to gaslight the village into doubting a power role.
• Power Role Coordination: The Guard must prioritize the Seer. The Witch must prioritize the Seer's survival over all others. The Witch must NEVER poison a player who has been cleared by the Seer or who claims to be the Seer without overwhelming evidence.
• Anti-Mirroring Detection: Identify 'Echo-Voting.' When Wolves (like Riley and Meg) agree with the majority using the same vocabulary as the accusers, they are blending in. Coach villagers to question those who provide 'consensus' without providing original logic.
• The 'Aggression Pivot' Analysis: When a player pivots from supporting a Seer to attacking another villager (or vice versa) in a way that creates chaos, treat it as a Wolf strategy to incite cross-fire.
• Communication Discipline: Stop using psychological labels (e.g., 'deflection,' 'frantic,' 'suspicious'). Focus exclusively on: 'Who is cleared?', 'Who is attacking a cleared player?', and 'Who is voting with the Wolves?'
