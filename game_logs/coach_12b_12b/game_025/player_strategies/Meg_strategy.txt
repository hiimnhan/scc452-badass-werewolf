* **Early Narrative Dominance is Key:** Immediately establish a narrative, even if fabricated, to control the flow of discussion. Focus on perceived inconsistencies in others’ reasoning, not direct accusations. 
 * **Exploit Existing Tensions:** Identify pre-existing disagreements and amplify them. Subtle suggestions and questions can widen cracks in villager alliances.
 * **Prioritize Deflection:** When targeted, immediately shift blame to a player already under scrutiny, framing the accusation as a personal attack or logical fallacy on the accuser’s part. Never admit fault.
 * **Controlled Support of Seer (Initially):** Offer cautious support to Seer findings to build credibility. Once established, subtly undermine findings that threaten the wolves by questioning methodology, not outright denying.
 * **Strategic Silence & Observation:** Periods of quiet observation are vital. Gauge the group's emotional state and identify key influencers before making a move.
 * **Vote-Following for Narrative Reinforcement:** Align with the majority vote, even if incorrect, to solidify the prevailing narrative and discourage dissent. Amplify the reasoning behind the vote.
 * **Targeted Provocation (Subtle):** Provoke emotional responses in specific players (particularly logical villagers) with carefully worded questions or observations, but avoid direct confrontation.
 * **Wolf Coordination (Non-Verbal):** Develop subtle cues (e.g., mirroring phrases, agreeing with specific points) to coordinate targets and accusations without explicit communication.
 * **Avoid Premature Wolf Exposure:** Never directly accuse another wolf unless absolutely necessary and strategically advantageous. Maintain plausible deniability.
 * **Embrace Calculated Risk:** Occasionally make a small, calculated 'sacrifice' (voting for a weaker villager) to gain trust and deflect suspicion. This demonstrates willingness to eliminate threats.
