KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Behavioral Voting: The village exiled the real Seer in Round 1 because his tone was perceived as 'manipulative,' ignoring the mechanical value of his clear. 2. Lack of Power Role Synergy: The Guard failed to protect the Seer, and the Witch actively killed the Guard, destroying the village's defensive infrastructure. 3. Failure to Identify the 'Blend-in' Tactic: The village focused on the loudest players while the Wolves safely voted with the majority to eliminate power roles. 4. Treatment of the 'Clear': The village viewed a Seer's clear as 'unsubstantiated' rather than a primary piece of evidence, allowing the Wolves to sow doubt successfully.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop exiling players based on 'aggressive behavior' or 'suspicious vibes.' These are subjective markers that Wolves use to manipulate you into killing your own power roles. Logic must be based on role claims and mechanical results. When a Seer provides a clear, that person and the Seer form a 'Confirmed Core'; any player attacking this core without a counter-claim is a high-probability Wolf.

Seer: You were exiled because you didn't aggressively establish your authority. When you provide a clear, demand that the village treat it as a mechanical fact unless a counter-claim is made.

Guard: Your priority is to protect the Seer. In Round 1, you protected a Villager instead of the Seer. Once the Seer claims, they are the most valuable asset; protect them at all costs to ensure the village has a source of truth.

Witch: Your use of the poison potion on the Guard was a catastrophic error. Never poison a player based on 'suspicion.' Poison is a permanent resource to be used only on players with direct mechanical contradictions (e.g., two people claiming the same role). By poisoning the Guard, you did the Wolves' work for them.

All: Watch the 'Silent Majority.' Notice how the Wolves (Riley and Meg) blended into the crowd, voting for the Seer and the fake-Seer to maintain chaos. If a vote is based on 'behavioral' arguments rather than role-logic, the people quietly agreeing with the majority are often the Wolves.
