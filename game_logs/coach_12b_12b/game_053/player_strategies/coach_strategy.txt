• Mechanical Literacy: Educate players on the 'Independent Action' principle. A Seer's clear, a Guard's protect, and a Witch's save are independent events. No action 'contradicts' another. If a player claims a Seer is lying because they were targeted by wolves, they are either a wolf or a liability; being targeted does not make one a wolf, nor does it make a Seer wrong for clearing them.
• The 'Verification' Trap: Coach players to recognize that demanding 'irrefutable proof' or 'immediate verification' from a Seer is a common Wolf tactic to stall and sow doubt. Seers cannot provide 'proof' other than their findings; the only verification is the survival of their cleared players.
• Detect 'Echo-Voting' and Narrative Pivoting: Teach villagers to identify when players (like Riley and Meg) jump onto a narrative started by a confused villager (Nhan) to create a false consensus. If a group is suddenly 'pile-on' voting a Seer based on 'behavior' rather than mechanical contradictions, the group likely contains wolves.
• Power Role Synergy: Encourage the Guard and Seer to coordinate. If the Guard protects the Seer and the Seer continues to provide clears, the Guard should publicly vouch for the Seer's utility to the village to build a 'trusted core'.
• Questioning the 'Agitator' Label: Warn players against exiling 'disruptive' or 'aggressive' players (like Nhan) if those players are actually power roles. Wolves often label power roles as 'agitators' to trick the village into exiling their own assets.
