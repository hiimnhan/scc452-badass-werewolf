KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Disregarding the Seer: The village exiled the Seer in Round 1 despite him providing a valid clear. Behavioral suspicion was prioritized over objective role information.
2. Role Mismanagement: The Guard protected a cleared villager instead of the Seer, leaving the village's only source of truth vulnerable.
3. Logical Fallacies: The village relied entirely on 'deflection' and 'mirroring' accusations. Since no one demanded mechanical proof, the wolves were able to blend in by simply agreeing with the prevailing 'vibe' of the debate.
4. Failure to Form a Power Block: The Witch and Guard failed to publicly align with the Seer, allowing the wolves to sow discord and isolate the power roles.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop using 'vibe-based' logic. Terms like 'feels manufactured' or 'smells of coordination' are useless and allow wolves to manipulate you. Demand mechanical evidence. If a player is cleared by the Seer, they are an objective asset; exiling them based on 'behavior' is a catastrophic loss of information.

Seer: When you clear a player, state it as a hard fact. If the village begins to doubt you based on 'behavioral patterns,' call out the lack of mechanical evidence immediately. You are the only source of objective truth.

Guard: Your priority is the Seer. Protecting a cleared villager while the Seer is alive is a wasted action. Once the Seer is out, you have no compass; keep them alive at all costs.

Witch: You must coordinate with the Seer. When the Seer clears a player, announce your save to create a 'Confirmed Block' of safe players. This anchors the village and prevents the chaos seen in this game.

All: Identify the 'Lead Accuser.' In this game, you fell into an echo chamber where players simply mirrored accusations of 'deflection.' When this happens, stop and ask: 'What mechanical proof does the lead accuser have?' If there is none, the entire chain of suspicion is invalid.
