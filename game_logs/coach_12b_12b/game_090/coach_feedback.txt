KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. The Guard failed to protect the Seer after the reveal, leaving the village's only source of truth vulnerable. 2. The Villagers succumbed to 'Wolf Mirroring,' where Wolves (Riley and Meg) used the language of 'verification' and 'scrutiny' to cast doubt on the Seer's objective findings. 3. The Witch committed a game-ending error by poisoning Hoang, a villager, rather than targeting suspected wolves. 4. The village voted out their own Seer based on behavioral suspicion rather than factual evidence, effectively handing the win to the Werewolves.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: Your data was objective, but your delivery was perceived as too aggressive. In future games, frame your 'Confirmed Core' as a tool for the village rather than a mandate. Villagers: You prioritized 'behavioral vibes' over factual data. When a Seer provides a clear, the burden of proof shifts to the accuser. Anyone attacking a cleared player using vague logic (e.g., 'suspicious core-building') is likely a Wolf mirroring logical language to mislead you. Guard: Your primary mission is Seer protection. Once the Seer reveals themselves and clears a player, you must protect the Seer immediately. Protecting others while the Seer is exposed is a critical failure. Witch: Never poison a player who has been cleared by the Seer or who is supporting a verified power role without a direct counter-claim. Your poison on a villager accelerated the Wolves' victory. All Villagers: In the day debate, use your bidding to ensure the Seer's information is the focal point. If the Seer is under attack by players using 'process' arguments, override your gut feeling and vote with the evidence.
