KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Exiling a cleared player: The village exiled the Witch despite the Seer's clear, based on the flawed logic that the Witch was being 'disruptive.'
2. Failure to protect the Seer: The Guard protected the Witch and then a Villager, leaving the Seer vulnerable to the Wolves' targeted attack.
3. Behavioral Bias: The village focused on 'tone' and 'mannerisms' rather than the objective truth provided by the Seer, allowing the Wolves to gaslight the group into exiling the Seer in Round 2.
4. Poor Coordination: The special roles acted in isolation. There was no strategy to shield the Seer or leverage the Witch's potions to ensure the Seer's longevity.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Villagers: Stop prioritizing 'behavioral' arguments over objective facts. When the Seer clears a player, that player is a confirmed asset. Attacking a cleared player for being 'disruptive' is a classic Wolf tactic to remove village power. If someone calls a clear 'unverified' or 'concerning,' they are your primary suspects. Evidence always overrides 'vibes.'

Seer: Your reporting was correct, but your tone became too aggressive in the debate. Avoid calling others 'textbook' or 'calculated' as this allows Wolves to frame you as unstable. State your findings neutrally and let the facts do the work.

Guard: Your priority must be the Seer. Protecting the Witch in Night 1 was a waste of a move; the Seer is the most valuable target. Once the Seer is revealed, protect them every single night until they are no longer the primary target.

Witch: You used your save potion early on a Villager. While this prevented a death, the save potion is your most powerful tool to keep the Seer alive. Prioritize the Seer's survival above all else.

All: Be wary of the 'Mirror Tactic.' When a player accuses the Seer of 'deflecting' or 'manufacturing outrage,' they are usually projecting. Identify these keywords as red flags for Werewolves.
