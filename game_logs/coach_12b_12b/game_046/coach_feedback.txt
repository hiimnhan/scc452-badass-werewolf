KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Role Data Ignored: The village exiled the Witch in Round 1 despite the Seer's clear of the Guard, simply because the Witch was 'too supportive.' This allowed the Wolves to eliminate a powerful role based on a fake behavioral narrative.
2. Mismanaged Resources: The Witch used the save potion on themselves instead of the Seer, leaving the village vulnerable to the loss of information.
3. Wolf-Bandwagoning: The village fell for the Wolves' tactic of mirroring the crowd. The Wolves pushed the narrative that the Witch was 'steering' the conversation, and the Villagers followed along without logical contradiction.
4. Failure to Protect the Seer: While the Guard eventually protected the Seer in Round 2, the lack of a coordinated 'Seer-Shield' strategy in Round 1 left the village fragmented.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
To the Villagers: Stop prioritizing 'vibes' and behavioral analysis over hard role data. In Round 1, the Seer provided a confirmed clear on the Guard. Instead of using this as a foundation, the village exiled the Witch because they were 'acting too eager.' A Seer's clear is the gold standard; any player attacking a cleared player or the Seer should be your primary suspect. 

To the Witch: You wasted your save potion on yourself in Round 1. While self-preservation is intuitive, your priority must be the Seer. If the Seer dies, the village loses its only source of truth. Save the Seer at all costs.

To the Guard: You protected a Wolf in Round 1. While you cannot know for sure, protecting the Seer or the most vocal villager is generally safer than guessing. Use the Seer's information to narrow down who NOT to protect.

To the Seer: You did your job by providing clears, but you failed to lead the village logically. When the village began voting for the Witch based on 'suspicious behavior,' you should have more aggressively argued that a confirmed clear (the Guard) is a factual anchor that overrides psychological guesses. 

General Strategy: Be wary of 'Narrative Steering.' The Wolves successfully framed the Witch and a Villager by using vague terms like 'forced' and 'deflective.' Replace 'He's acting weird' with 'He is attacking a player the Seer confirmed as a villager.' That is a logical proof, not a feeling.
