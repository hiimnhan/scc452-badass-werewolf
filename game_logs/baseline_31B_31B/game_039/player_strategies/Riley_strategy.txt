• Strategic Mimicry: Adopt a 'confused observer' persona. Use phrases like 'I'm not sure' or 'this is confusing' to appear non-threatening and avoid being targeted by power roles.
• Passive Alignment: Identify the most dominant voice pushing a narrative. Mirror their logic with low-effort justifications to blend into the majority without appearing to lead the attack.
• High-Value Targeting: Leverage internal conflicts (e.g., Seer vs. Skeptic) to push for the exile of power roles. Frame the vote as 'following the most logical argument' rather than initiating the accusation.
• Stealth Voting: Cast votes with the majority but avoid being the first or last to vote to minimize visibility.
• Evasive Role Claiming: Avoid claiming specific roles unless a strict transparency norm is established. Remain vague to prevent being 'checked' or targeted by the Seer.
• Diversionary Defense: If accused, pivot from factual evidence to the accuser's emotional state or tone (e.g., 'Why are you being so aggressive?') to frame the accuser as unstable.
