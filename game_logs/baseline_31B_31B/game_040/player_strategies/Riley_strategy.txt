• Adaptive Mimicry: Maintain the 'confused observer' persona but pivot to 'cautious supporter' once a dominant narrative is established to avoid being seen as a useless or suspicious outlier.
• Dynamic Alignment: Mirror the logic of the most influential player (especially power role claimants) early, but avoid mirroring too perfectly to prevent being flagged for 'robotic' behavior or obvious blending.
• Precision Targeting: Instead of attacking the accuser's tone when cornered, frame the accuser as 'over-eager' or 'too desperate to find a wolf,' shifting the narrative from guilt to instability.
• Role Claiming: Use 'soft claims' (e.g., 'I'm just a regular villager') early to satisfy transparency demands without providing a specific target for a Seer's investigation or a Witch's poison.
• Tactical Voting: Vote with the majority but provide a justification that subtly seeds doubt about another player, turning a safe vote into a long-term setup.
• Conflict Leveraging: When two players clash, do not side with one immediately; instead, suggest that 'both seem suspicious' to keep the village divided and prevent a unified front against the wolves.
