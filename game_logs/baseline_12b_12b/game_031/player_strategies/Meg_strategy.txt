**Prioritize Villager-on-Villager Conflict:** The initial rounds demonstrated the effectiveness of turning villagers against each other. This remains the top priority.
**Early Narrative Control:** Immediately establish a dominant narrative, even if fabricated. Use strong, declarative statements to shape perceptions. Avoid nuanced arguments.
**Leverage Emotional Responses:** Target players exhibiting emotional reactions (anger, defensiveness). Amplify their inconsistencies and misinterpretations.
**Controlled Agreement, Gradual Steering:** Agree with the prevailing sentiment to appear cooperative. Subtly introduce doubts about alternative targets.
**False Accusations as Diversions:** Wrongly accusing villagers is acceptable, but ensure accusations are plausible and create maximum disruption.
**Silence as a Tool:** Use strategic silence to appear thoughtful or neutral, especially when under scrutiny. Respond minimally to probing questions.
**Exploit Perceived Logic:** Identify players who value logic and reason. Feed them carefully crafted narratives that subtly implicate others.
**False Alliances (Opportunistic):** Form superficial alliances only when strategically beneficial, ready to betray them at the opportune moment.
**Observe Voting Patterns Meticulously:** Identify voting blocs and inconsistencies. Use this information to predict behavior and manipulate votes.
**Weaponize Misinterpretation:** *Never* correct a player's misinterpretation. Frame it as evidence of their flawed judgment.
**Guard/Seer Targeting (Opportunistic):** Only target Guard/Seer if they pose an immediate threat to our strategy or are creating excessive chaos. Otherwise, focus on villager infighting.
**Riley Communication (Refined):** When Riley is present, use single-word responses with subtle inflection (e.g., 'Focus,' 'Observe,' 'Question') to guide suspicion without direct accusation.
**Proactive Narrative Framing:** Anticipate potential suspicions and proactively frame our actions as defensive measures.
