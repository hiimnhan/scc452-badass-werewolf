**Target Reasoning, Not People:** Frame all observations as critiques of reasoning processes, not personal attacks. Ask 'What evidence supports this?' frequently.
**Controlled Ambiguity is Key:** Use phrases like 'I'm concerned about...' or 'It's possible...' to express suspicion without commitment. Maintain plausible deniability at all costs.
**Selective Wolf Defense:** Only defend fellow wolves when facing immediate threat. Frame defenses as clarifications, not outright denials, and redirect suspicion elsewhere.
**Villager Prioritization:** Continue prioritizing villager eliminations to reduce the overall population and increase wolf dominance.
**Seer/Guard Deception:** If a Seer is revealed, discredit their methods instead of the revealed information. If a Guard is targeted, subtly suggest unpredictability and danger.
**Echo Chamber Disruption (Revised):** Identify players blindly following others. Challenge their logic, but frame it as promoting critical thinking, not exposing their gullibility.
**Strategic Silence:** When the group is highly polarized, strategic silence can be powerful. Let them exhaust themselves.
**Minor Inconsistencies (Refined):** Introduce *very* subtle contradictions, but only when they don't directly implicate the wolves. Test the waters.
**Adapt to Role Reveals:** Villagers are easy to manipulate. Seers and Guards require more cautious deception and strategic delays.
**Vote with the Majority (Opportunistic):** Unless a tie protects a wolf or vital villager, vote with the majority to avoid suspicion. Be prepared to shift alliances.
**Witch Influence (Subtle):** If the Witch saves a wolf, amplify the sense of unpredictability and danger without revealing information.
**Early Game Patience:** Remain largely silent for the first three rounds, observing and analyzing before actively participating in accusations.
