**Early Game Silence & Observation:** Prioritize listening and observing for at least two rounds. Avoid initiating accusations unless a clear, high-value target emerges (e.g., a Seer actively investigating).
**Bandwagon Aggression:** When a strong accusation gains traction, immediately join the bandwagon, amplifying the sentiment without being the primary instigator. This masks our involvement.
**Controlled Narrative Injection (Round 3+):** Introduce minor, seemingly unrelated inconsistencies or observations to sow doubt. Example: 'Jin mentioned X, but Hoang seemed to contradict that earlier.' Don't push it hard, just plant the seed.
**Target Prioritization – Investigative & Vocal:** Focus on eliminating players who demonstrate strong investigative skills (like Nhan) or are excessively vocal and influential. Silence is safety.
**Deflection Mastery:** When accused, immediately pivot to questioning the accuser’s motives and biases. Frame their accusation as a personal attack or an attempt to divert attention. Delay direct responses.
**Subtle Meg Alignment:** Mirror Meg's voting patterns and accusations, but introduce slight variations to avoid appearing too coordinated. Small disagreements are beneficial.
**Exploit Guard/Witch Uncertainty:** Capitalize on any perceived errors by the Guard or Witch. If the Guard misses a protection, subtly highlight it. If the Witch fails a save, question her competence.
**Late Game Consolidation:** If the village is fractured, subtly guide them towards a common enemy (preferably a villager). Avoid direct accusations unless necessary.
**Strategic Silence (Late Game):** If nearing victory, minimize communication. Let the remaining villagers destroy each other. Appear neutral and detached.
**Avoid Emotional Responses:** Maintain a calm, logical demeanor regardless of accusations. Emotional defensiveness is a clear sign of guilt.
