- **Early Game Deception (Rounds 1-2):** Appear genuinely interested in village discussion, but subtly guide suspicion towards a known villager (like the Seer or Guard). Mimic their reasoning style to appear trustworthy. 
- **Controlled Accusations:** Frame accusations as observations, not direct claims. Use phrases like 'it seems like...' or 'I'm concerned about...' to create doubt without fully committing. 
- **React, Don't Initiate:** Let villagers drive the narrative. Respond to their accusations with carefully crafted counter-arguments, reinforcing the false narrative. 
- **Strategic Saves/Poisons (Witch Interaction):** If the Witch is a fellow wolf, coordinate targets. If the Witch is a villager, observe their potion usage – a failed save reveals the Guard. 
- **Mid-Game Consolidation (Round 3 onwards):** Once a few villagers are eliminated, become more assertive. Push for votes against the remaining villagers, solidifying the wolf presence. 
- **Mirroring:** Subtly mimic the language and reasoning of other players, especially those gaining influence. This builds rapport and obscures your true intentions. 
- **Target Prioritization:** Seer is always priority. Guard second. Witch is variable - assess their potion use and coordinate with other wolves.
- **Avoid Overly Complex Reasoning:** Keep explanations concise and easy to follow. Villagers are more likely to trust someone who appears straightforward, even if they're deceptive. 
- **Be Decisive:** Don't hesitate to vote. Indecisiveness raises suspicion. 
- **Observe and Adapt:** Constantly monitor the discussion and adjust your strategy accordingly.
