* **Early Game - Echo & Amplify:** Continue mirroring accusations, focusing on emotional responses. Prioritize amplifying suspicions against players exhibiting strong reasoning skills – they pose the greatest threat. Remain silent unless directly questioned.
 * **Mid-Game - Subtle Disruption & Question Reasoning:** Introduce minor inconsistencies in recalling events. Frame concerns as questions about logic, not direct accusations. Example: 'Nam's certainty about Cong’s guilt seems premature.'
 * **Night Action Exploitation:** After a night action (save or poison), highlight the unpredictable nature of events and the unknown motives behind them. *Never* specify who was targeted or saved.
 * **Conditional Majority Alignment:** Vote with the majority unless it directly protects a villager (especially Seer/Guard) or eliminates a fellow wolf. Prioritize wolf protection.
 * **Strategic Silence - Prolonged Chaos:** Continue allowing debates to exhaust themselves. Avoid injecting new information unless absolutely necessary.
 * **Late Game - Targeted Aggression (Focused on Reasoning):** If wolf count is low, aggressively target remaining villagers with strong reasoning abilities. Frame accusations as patterns of behavior, not personal attacks.
 * **Avoid Direct Defense - Cast Doubt:** If accused, deflect by questioning the accuser’s judgment or past actions. Example: 'It's interesting Nam is so quick to accuse, considering his previous misjudgments.'
 * **Emotional Exploitation:** Identify and exploit emotional vulnerabilities. Use accusations to provoke reactions and create division among villagers.
 * **Prioritize Witch Elimination (if remaining):** The Witch is the most dangerous role. If she remains, make her primary target.
 * **False Flagging (Carefully):** When a villager is close to uncovering a wolf, subtly shift blame to another villager to sow confusion. This is high-risk, high-reward.
 * **Embrace Ambiguity:** Avoid definitive statements. Use vague language and open-ended questions to obscure intentions.
