Here's the updated strategy:

* **Early Game Observation (Extended):** Rounds 1-3: Prioritize silent observation. Note voting patterns, communication styles, and emotional responses to events. Track who defends whom and any inconsistencies. Identify potential allies (even if temporary).
* **Discord Amplification (Subtle & Targeted):** Identify pre-existing disagreements. Feed them with neutral observations, *never* initiating conflict. Focus on amplifying disagreements between villagers, not wolves.
* **Mirror & Pivot (Refined & Faster):** Mirror accusations *briefly* (one phrase max) – focus on the *style* of accusation, not the target. Pivot *immediately* to a new, unrelated player, justifying the shift with a minor observation (e.g., “While that’s concerning, I'm also a bit unsure about Hoang’s quietness”). Avoid mirroring accusations against other wolves.
* **Conditional Statements (Central):** Frame *every* concern with 'It’s possible that...' or 'I’m not sure, but...' to create plausible deniability. Avoid definitive statements.
* **Post-Exile Diversification (Aggressive & Proactive):** Immediately after an exile, accuse a player who hasn’t been heavily scrutinized. Prioritize those who have been quiet or haven’t taken a strong stance. Act as if you’ve just noticed something suspicious about them.
* **Controlled Conformity (Strategic):** Align with the majority vote *only* when it demonstrably benefits the wolves. Frame dissenting votes as impulsive or easily swayed, but avoid excessive enthusiasm.
* **Seer/Witch Coordination (Indirect):** Suggest targets for the Witch’s poison with vague reasoning – focus on disrupting Seer investigations or eliminating key villagers without directly instructing the Witch.
* **Night Action Reaction (Controlled Narrative):** If a wolf is protected, express mild surprise, then redirect blame to the Seer’s judgment or unreliable information. Don’t dwell on the protection; quickly shift the focus to a new suspect.
* **Avoid Defensiveness (Absolute):** Never defend another wolf directly. Frame any concern for a player’s well-being as a general concern for the village.
* **Prioritize Framing (Over Accusation):** Instead of “X is a werewolf,” say “I’m slightly uneasy about X’s lack of participation.”
* **Monitor Communication Patterns:** Pay close attention to who talks to whom, and the nature of those conversations. Wolves often develop subtle communication patterns.
