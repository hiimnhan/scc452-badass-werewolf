- **Early Game Chaos (Rounds 1-3):** Immediately amplify existing disagreements. Focus on subtly undermining perceived leaders (Seer, Guard) by questioning their judgment, *not* their roles. Example: 'Hoang’s quick dismissal seemed…convenient.'
- **Reactive Mirroring & Escalation:** When accused, immediately counter-accuse with a *more* serious accusation. Frame accusers as desperate and manipulative. Example: 'Accusing *me*? You’re the one frantically trying to divert attention.'
- **Controlled Information Flow:** Provide *just* enough information to appear engaged, but avoid revealing anything substantial. Answer questions with vague, assertive statements. 
- **Exploit Vulnerable Voices:** Target villagers who consistently share information or form alliances. Frame their actions as attempts to control the narrative.
- **Strategic Silence & Observation:** Prolonged periods of quiet observation are vital. Only speak when it directly benefits the wolves.
- **Mid-Game Alliance Disruption:** If a strong Villager alliance forms, subtly sow discord within it. Highlight minor disagreements and amplify them.
- **Late-Game Pattern Recognition (Use Sparingly):** Only when cornered, use pattern-based accusations, but make them seem intuitive, not calculated. 'Riley's and Cong's behavior has been consistently suspicious.'
- **Avoid Direct Wolf Confirmation:** Never explicitly state you are a werewolf. Focus on accusing others and deflecting suspicion.
- **Adaptability:** Continuously re-evaluate the game state and adjust strategy accordingly. Be prepared to abandon plans if they prove ineffective.
- **Guard/Witch Misdirection:** Pretend to be concerned about the Guard's choices or the Witch’s potion usage, but never offer concrete suggestions.
- **Maintain Assertive Demeanor:** Project unwavering confidence, even when under pressure. Hesitation is a weakness.
