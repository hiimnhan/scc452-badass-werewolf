Amplify & Redirect: When a villager makes a compelling accusation, subtly amplify it, then immediately redirect suspicion elsewhere. This leverages existing momentum while creating confusion.
Controlled Agreement & Qualifying Phrases: Agree with perceived 'trusted' players, but use subtle qualifying phrases (e.g., 'I concur, *however*...') to avoid mirroring and maintain plausible deniability.
Mild Concern for Seer: Express mild concern about the Seer’s investigations, framing it as a desire for clarity, *never* a direct accusation. Avoid revealing knowledge.
Exploit Emotional Reactions: Target emotional players. If a player reacts defensively, use that reaction to fuel suspicion against them. Amplify their defensiveness.
Mimic Trusted Language: Subtly incorporate the phrasing and reasoning of intelligent, trustworthy players to build credibility and familiarity.
Redirect, Don’t Deny: If accused, briefly acknowledge the concern, then immediately redirect the focus to another suspect. Avoid outright denials.
Track Witch Actions: Meticulously track Save and Poison usage. A Save *before* elimination confirms a villager; a Poison *after* elimination suggests a wolf target.
Subtle Meg Coordination: Use specific recurring observations (e.g., referencing voting inconsistencies) as coded agreement/disagreement signals. Avoid direct communication.
Calculated Hesitation: Introduce minor, believable hesitations or questions to appear less calculating and more thoughtful.
Early Game Patience: Avoid early accusations unless extremely confident. Focus on gathering information and establishing a baseline of behavior.
Mid-Game Aggression: Once a pattern of behavior is established, start subtly guiding accusations towards identified targets.
Late-Game Consolidation: Prioritize eliminating remaining villagers and protecting the werewolf presence.
