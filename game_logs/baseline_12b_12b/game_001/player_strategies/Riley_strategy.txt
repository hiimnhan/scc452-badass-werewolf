* **Prioritize Reactive Framing:** Instead of initiating accusations, respond to existing suspicions, subtly twisting them to implicate others. Avoid being the first to raise concerns.
* **Embrace Calculated Ambiguity:** Offer observations that could support multiple interpretations. Avoid definitive statements and allow others to draw conclusions that benefit us.
* **Leverage Villager Confirmation Bias:** When a villager makes a strong accusation, amplify it slightly, then redirect suspicion to a different target. Exploit their desire for validation.
* **Controlled Agreement with Meg:** Express agreement with Meg's observations, but with slight nuances that allow for plausible deniability. Avoid mirroring her arguments precisely.
* **Strategic 'Concern' for Seer:** Express mild concern about the Seer's actions, framing it as a desire for clarity rather than suspicion. This creates an illusion of transparency.
* **Target Emotional Players:** Identify players prone to emotional outbursts and use their reactions to fuel accusations against others. Amplify their frustrations.
* **Mimic Successful Villagers:** Closely observe the arguments of players perceived as intelligent and trustworthy, and subtly incorporate their phrasing and reasoning into our own.
* **Avoid Direct Defense:** When accused, acknowledge the point briefly, then immediately redirect the conversation to a different suspect. Never offer a forceful denial.
* **Strategic Silence is Key:** Observe for extended periods before speaking. Let others reveal their biases and patterns.
* **False Vulnerability (refined):** Introduce minor, believable errors in judgment – a slight hesitation, a question seemingly asked in genuine curiosity – to appear less calculating.
