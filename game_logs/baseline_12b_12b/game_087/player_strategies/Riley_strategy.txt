* **Early Game Silence, Targeted Observation:** Rounds 1-2: Absolute silence unless a clear opportunity arises. Focus entirely on observing voting patterns, accusation styles, and responses to events. Note who defends whom, and any inconsistencies. 
* **Leverage Pre-Existing Discord:** Identify any initial disagreements or mistrust among villagers. Subtly amplify these, never initiating conflict, but feeding existing tensions. 
* **Mirror & Rapid Pivot (Refined):** Mirror accusations briefly (1 sentence max), then pivot *immediately* to a new, unrelated target. The pivot must be logical, even if flimsy, to avoid suspicion. Avoid mirroring accusations against other wolves. 
* **Embrace Ambiguity & Conditional Statements (Prioritized):** Frame *all* concerns with 'It's possible that...' or 'I'm not sure, but...' This creates plausible deniability and avoids direct commitment. 
* **Post-Exile Diversification (Aggressive):** After each exile, accuse a player who has received minimal scrutiny. Don't revisit previous suspects. Prioritize players who haven't spoken much.
* **Controlled Conformity (Selective):** Only align with the majority vote when it significantly advances the wolf victory. Frame dissenting votes as impulsive or easily swayed, but avoid appearing overly enthusiastic.
* **Seer Manipulation (Subtle):** If the Witch is cooperative, subtly suggest targets for poison that disrupt the Seer’s investigations or eliminate key villagers, but without direct instruction.
* **React to Night Actions (Calculated):** If a wolf is protected, express mild surprise, then immediately redirect blame to the Seer's perceived misjudgment or unreliable information. Don't dwell on the protection.
* **Avoid Defensiveness:** Never directly defend another wolf. Any defense should be framed as a general concern for the village’s well-being.
* **Prioritize Misdirection over Direct Accusation:** Frame concerns rather than accusations. For example, instead of 'Jin is a werewolf', say 'I'm slightly uneasy about Jin’s silence.'
