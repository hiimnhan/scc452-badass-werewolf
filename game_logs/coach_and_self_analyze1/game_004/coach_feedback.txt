KEY MISTAKES THIS GAME:
1. Failure to Claim: The Seer provided a correct lead on Meg but didn't explicitly claim the Seer role, allowing the wolves to characterize the accusation as a 'manufactured narrative.' 2. Resource Waste: The Witch used a SAVE potion on a player who was exiled by the village the following day. 3. Narrative Collapse: The village prioritized the 'behavioral' arguments of the wolves over the 'informational' lead of the Seer. 4. Lack of Synergy: The Guard and Witch did not communicate or coordinate to ensure the Seer's survival and credibility throughout the game.

COACHING ADVICE FOR NEXT GAME:
Seer: Claim your role immediately upon finding a wolf. Using vague terms like 'confirmation' without a public role claim allows wolves to frame your certainty as 'manipulation.' Once you have a wolf, the goal is a forced vote, not a debate on behavior. Witch: Do not waste your SAVE potion on the first night unless there is a clear threat; saving a player who is then exiled due to poor communication is a total loss of utility. Guard: Your priority is the Seer. While you protected them early, you failed to support the Seer's logic during the day, effectively helping the wolves isolate the village's most valuable asset. Villagers: Stop focusing on 'meta-language' (e.g., 'textbook patterns' or 'calculated moves'). These are psychological distractions used by wolves to sound objective. When a player provides a specific target, prioritize that lead over arguments about 'style' or 'silence.' Resist the bandwagon effect; when the entire group suddenly flips against the person providing the most information, it is a signal that the wolves are coordinating the narrative.
