**Night Phase**

*   **Early Game (Round 1-2):** Prioritize saving players demonstrating strong reasoning and investigative skills. Avoid poisoning unless there's overwhelming evidence. Saving Nhan in round 1 was ineffective due to the witch’s simultaneous poison.
*   **Mid-Game (Round 3+):** If a verifiable Seer exists, protect them consistently. Otherwise, prioritize protecting active investigators targeted by wolves. If a strong villager investigator is targeted, save them.
*   **Double Kill:** If a double kill occurs with no information, save the first targeted player to observe reactions. This proved ineffective in this game.

**Day Discussion**

*   **Seer Claims:** Demand immediate, verifiable results. Vague claims or avoidance of direct questions warrant extreme suspicion. Hoang’s rapid accusation of Cong lacked sufficient justification.
*   **Deflection Patterns:** Aggressive attacks without evidence indicate potential wolf behavior. Question reasoning behind accusations.
*   **Benefit Analysis:** After each exile, identify beneficiaries. Wolves often manipulate exiles to protect themselves.
*   **Role Accusations:** Demand concrete evidence supporting claims. False claims are strong wolf indicators.
*   **Suspect Clusters:** Aggressive defense of each other is suspicious. Analyze reasoning and look for coordination.

**Voting Phase**

*   **Prioritize Wolf Elimination:** Base votes on strongest evidence, regardless of perceived utility. A false Seer is less dangerous than an undetected wolf.
*   **Tie-Breaking:** Prioritize eliminating the player who benefits most from the survival of tied players.
*   **Avoid Vote Splitting:** Unless necessary, avoid splitting votes.
*   **Post-Save Analysis:** Analyze who was saved and subtly communicate this to the village.

**General Principles**

*   **Observation:** Diligently observe player statements and actions.
*   **Adaptability:** Adjust strategy based on game dynamics. 
*   **Neutrality:** Maintain a neutral demeanor.
*   **Narrative Consistency:** Refer back to earlier statements to expose inconsistencies.
