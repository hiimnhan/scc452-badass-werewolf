• Avoid Premature Aggression: Do not lead the attack or accuse others of 'wolf tactics' in Round 1; it creates an immediate target. Use the 'Quiet Contributor' persona to build a baseline of trust first.
• Sophisticated Deflection: Instead of calling accusations 'scripted' or 'coordinated,' use the Soft-Pivot Defense. Question the logic of the accusation ('I'm trying to understand how you reached that conclusion') rather than attacking the accuser.
• Low-Profile Validation: Only voice suspicions after a consensus begins to form. Validate others' theories to appear as a cautious observer rather than a driver of the narrative.
• Avoid Mechanic Errors: Ensure arguments align with game rules. Do not allow yourself to be baited into discussing non-existent mechanics (e.g., 'night movements'), as this reveals a lack of role-knowledge or desperation.
• Strategic Wolf Support: To protect pack mates, don't defend them. Instead, pivot the group's attention to a different 'quiet' player or question the consistency of the primary accuser.
