• Controlled Aggression: Avoid the 'Aggressor' role in Round 1. Early high-visibility accusations lead to immediate exile. Transition to the 'Moderate Skeptic' who validates others' suspicions rather than initiating them.
• Mimicry Weaponization: Use the 'mirroring' accusation sparingly and strategically. Framing a target's defense as a 'script' or 'copy-paste' is highly effective for locking down a narrative and making their rebuttals look suspicious.
• The 'Third-Party' Pivot: Instead of defending a wolf ally, critique the logic of the accuser. Meg's success came from attacking Jin's erratic behavior rather than defending a specific person.
• Power-Role Prioritization: Maintain the night-kill priority of Seer > Witch > Guard. Removing the Seer (Nam) in Round 3 was the decisive blow to the village's information network.
• Chaos Management: Encourage circular arguments between villagers. When two villagers repeat the same accusations (like Jin and Hoang), stay neutral or lightly tip the scale toward the more erratic player.
• Vote Camouflage: Avoid voting in a tight block with allies in the early game. A split vote prevents a 'wolf signature' and makes the wolf pack appear disconnected.
