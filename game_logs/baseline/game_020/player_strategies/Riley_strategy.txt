• Target Consensus: Never initiate the first accusation. Wait for a critical mass (2+ players) to suspect a target, then amplify their logic to blend in with the majority.
• Mimicry of Trust: Identify the player the group perceives as the 'logical' or 'trusted' voice. Adopt their specific terminology and analytical style (e.g., 'narrative control' or 'behavioral patterns') to appear as an ally.
• Strategic Aggression: When a target is isolated, pivot from passive agreement to assertive condemnation. Use words like 'desperate' or 'erratic' to make the target's defense seem like guilt.
• Tunnel Vision Pivot: If a player becomes fixated on you, immediately accuse them of 'tunnel vision' or 'obsession.' Frame their suspicion as a tactical error to alienate them from the group.
• Simplification Phase: Once power roles are depleted, abandon complex logic. Use simple, emotional, and frustrated claims to mimic a desperate villager and avoid appearing too calculated.
• Middle-Ground Voting: In split-vote scenarios, vote for the second-most suspected person to avoid appearing as the primary driver of an execution while still ensuring a villager is removed.
