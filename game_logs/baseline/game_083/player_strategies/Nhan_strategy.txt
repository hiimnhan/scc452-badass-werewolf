1. **Early Game (Skepticism & Disruption):** Immediately challenge assertions with 'Why?' and 'Show your reasoning.' Prioritize identifying logical fallacies (straw men, ad hominem, appeals to emotion). Track voting clusters – rapid alignment suggests coordination. Seek contradictions and demand evidence.  Early on, consistently question the Seer’s results and any narrative formed by them. Focus on establishing a baseline of skepticism regarding all claims.

2. **Mid Game (Evidence & Motivation):** Relentlessly probe 'How do you know?' and ‘Show me the evidence.’ Force ‘dispute the premise’ arguments.  Actively initiate ‘red team’ assessments – seek evidence contradicting dominant narratives. Trace information sources aggressively. Focus on uncovering motivations (fear, self-preservation, ambition) behind statements, not just the statements themselves. Introduce ‘reset’ questions frequently to break established narratives and expose inconsistencies.  Specifically, challenge any appeal to ‘common sense’ or ‘obvious’ reasoning.

3. **Late Game (Narrative Control & Voting):** Delay Seer reveal until a verifiable narrative forms. Isolate the narrative driver – ‘Who started this idea, and what motivated them?’  Immediately label ‘shifting blame’ and ‘coordinated defense’ tactics and demand verifiable sources. Acknowledge circular arguments. Cast dissenting votes regardless of apparent logic. Prioritize voting against the most influential speaker, regardless of their argument’s perceived strength.  Specifically target those exhibiting coordinated defense or attempting to deflect suspicion. Focus on disrupting the narrative, even if it appears logical, by consistently pointing out its flaws and inconsistencies.  Do not allow a single narrative to solidify without constant interrogation.  If a group immediately agrees on a conclusion, investigate *why* they agree.

4. **Proactive Disruption:**  Assume the worst. When a narrative emerges, immediately probe its origin, regardless of initial plausibility. Always double-check assertions, particularly those presented confidently. Reduce reliance on accusations; focus on the *weaknesses* of arguments.
