1. **Early Game - Immediate Skepticism & Narrative Control:** Maintain relentless questioning of all claims – ‘What evidence?’ – regardless of apparent neutrality. Immediately flag verbatim repetition and rapid agreement as coordination signals. Employ ‘reset’ questions (e.g., ‘Let’s start again – what’s the *actual* evidence?’) to dismantle unsupported narratives and disrupt attempts to steer the discussion. Prioritize identifying the initial narrative driver and interrupting their framing. Focus on establishing a baseline through direct questioning and demanding clear definitions. Introduce minor dissenting viewpoints early to create friction. Specifically target vague language and generalized statements, demanding concrete details.  Actively seek to identify speakers who consistently agree with others, regardless of the evidence. 
2. **Mid Game - Premise Decomposition & Disruption:** Continuously deconstruct assumptions – ‘What are you assuming?’ – and probe for motivations ( ‘What benefit do you gain?’). Actively seek counter-examples and expose logical fallacies (straw man arguments, ad hominem). Introduce dissenting viewpoints aggressively, even if seemingly minor, to sow doubt. When a ‘baseline’ is proposed, demand a clear definition and verifiable evidence. Immediately label instances of deflection, redirection, and ‘gaslighting’ – attempts to shift blame or discredit opposing arguments. Document repeated use of emotional appeals. 
3. **Late Game - Targeted Opposition & Seer Utilization:** Delay Seer reveal until a stable, cohesive narrative emerges. Immediately expose shifting blame and coordinated defense with specific, documented examples. Prioritize voting *against* the most influential speaker with precise justifications, focusing on inconsistencies and evasiveness. Utilize the Seer’s information strategically *only* when a stable narrative exists – don’t prematurely deploy it. Proactively expose attempts to manipulate the voting process (e.g., framing, appeals to emotion, false dilemmas). Aggressively challenge any attempts to reframe the debate or introduce irrelevant tangents.  Specifically target speakers who consistently frame their arguments as ‘common sense’ or ‘obvious’ – these are often used to mask coordination. Focus entirely on identifying and disrupting coordinated efforts, regardless of individual arguments.
