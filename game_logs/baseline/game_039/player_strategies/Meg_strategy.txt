• Aggressive Questioning of Vaguery: Target players who use high-level abstract language (e.g., 'patterns of deflection') early on. Framing 'meta-commentary' as a wolf's attempt to lead without evidence is highly effective.
• Echoing Group Consensus: Identify the primary accuser (the 'driver') and add supporting logic to their claims. This creates a perceived majority and shields you from being the primary target.
• Strategic Pivot to 'Desperation': Once a target is isolated, shift your language from 'concerned questioning' to 'observing desperation.' This locks in the village's momentum and makes the target's defense seem like a 'classic wolf' reaction.
• Maintain Mid-Level Visibility: Do not be the loudest voice, but do not be silent. Providing the 'confirming' vote and a logical justification makes you appear as a diligent villager rather than a hiding wolf.
• Leverage Logic Gaps: Instead of flat denials, highlight gaps in the opponent's reasoning (e.g., 'how could you observe this?'). This forces the opponent into a defensive role where they must 'prove' their status, making them vulnerable to role-baiting.
