• Refine Mirroring Accusations: Do not use 'mirroring' as a primary attack too early. When the village adopts the term, using it makes the user look like they are following a script. Use it sparingly to gaslight, not as a core accusation.
• Diversify Defensive Language: Avoid repetitive terms like 'railroading' or 'puppet master.' Shift to questioning the 'logic gaps' or 'unnatural consensus' to avoid being flagged as a predictable pattern.
• Timing of Aggression: Delay the pivot to assertive accusation. In Round 2, the transition from neutrality to high aggression was too abrupt, creating a 'wolf-like' spike in behavior. Maintain a baseline of moderate engagement.
• Strategic Concession: Occasionally agree with a weak point in a villager's argument to appear authentic and less defensive. Total denial of all suspicion is a red flag.
• Target Selection: Prioritize the 'Silent Support' players over the 'Narrative Controller' if the controller has already gained village trust. Attempting to take down the leader often consolidates the village against the attacker.
• Ally Shielding: If a teammate is being targeted, do not defend them directly. Instead, attack the accuser's methodology to create doubt without explicitly linking yourself to the suspect.
