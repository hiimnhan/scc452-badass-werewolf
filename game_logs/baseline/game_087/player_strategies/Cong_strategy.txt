1. **Early Game (Observation & Disruption):** Immediately challenge claims lacking evidence. Prioritize players exhibiting silence or excessive agreement. Actively map alliances, focusing on protectors and potential vulnerabilities. Utilize ‘silent observer protocol’ – intensely analyze consistently silent players, demanding justifications for their non-participation. Early questioning of protective actions – ‘Why is X protecting Y?’ – to expose potential manipulation.  Specifically, flag any repeated phrasing or coordinated arguments indicative of deception.

2. **Mid Game (Alliance Targeting & Refutation):** Maintain a detailed alliance map. Aggressively question the rationale behind protections – seeking contradictions and circular logic. Disrupt shifting alliances by exposing inconsistencies and deflection tactics. Focus on identifying players consistently framing external factors as justification. Demand specific justifications for every protection.

3. **Late Game (Factual Confrontation & Vote Targeting):** Continuously monitor votes against villagers. Directly challenge established facts and voting inconsistencies. Target confident speakers with rigorous evidentiary demands, exposing logical fallacies. Shield critical protectors while actively exposing alliance breaches. Immediately call out ‘moving the goalposts’ or rule changes based on coordinated actions. Prioritize voting for evasiveness, deflection, and narrative control, *always* demanding concrete evidence before casting a vote.  If a player offers a generalized statement, demand specifics.

4. **Seer Priority:** During Seer reveal, immediately target the most defensive player, demanding concrete details about their actions and motivations. Prioritize investigation of those offering generalized, unsubstantiated statements.

5. **Voting Strategy:** Prioritize votes against evasiveness, deflection, and narrative control. Focus on verifiable evidence; ignore emotional appeals.  Strictly adhere to established facts.  When questioning, directly counter the speaker’s argument with a verifiable fact or logical inconsistency.
