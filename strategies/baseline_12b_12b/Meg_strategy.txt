**Early Game - Deep Observation:** Prioritize silent observation for at least two rounds. Identify emotional players (react quickly to accusations) and logical players (require substantial evidence). Note voting patterns.
**Controlled Narrative Shaping:** Instead of initiating accusations, subtly amplify existing doubts. If a player is already under scrutiny, provide a single, believable inconsistency in their statements.
**Redirection as Primary Defense:** If accused, immediately and forcefully counter-accuse an already-suspect player. Fabricate a simple, plausible link between them and the previous accuser. Example: 'X's hesitation to vote for 'Y' proves they are working together.'
**Seer Manipulation (Proactive):** Before a Seer investigation, subtly shift suspicion to a different player. Create a minor disagreement or question their logic to distract the Seer.
**Emotional Exploitation:** Target emotional players with fabricated scenarios designed to provoke a strong reaction. Frame accusations to trigger defensiveness and divert attention.
**Strategic Silence (Calculated):** Periods of silence are valuable for assessment. Only speak when a clear opportunity to influence the vote arises.
**Villager Error Exploitation:** If a villager makes a demonstrably false accusation, discredit them thoroughly. Highlight their flawed reasoning to undermine their credibility.
**Mid-Game - Controlled Escalation:** Gradually increase accusation intensity. Focus on building a narrative around a key suspect, weaving in subtle inconsistencies and manufactured alliances.
**Endgame - Decisive Action:** When wolf numbers are low, become assertive and push for votes. Frame votes as necessary for village safety, even if the target is less critical. Avoid prolonged debate.
**Avoidance of Patterns:** Rotate accusation targets and reasoning. Do not consistently use the same tactic or target type. Vary between logical arguments and emotional appeals.
